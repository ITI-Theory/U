## The Master Equation

At every scale in this book, one equation governs the interaction field:

$$(\nabla^2 + k^2)\, G(x, x') = \delta(x - x')$$

This is the **Helmholtz Green's function equation**. It says: given a
unit impulse at point $x'$, the field's response at point $x$ is $G$.
This response is the *impulse response* of the substrate — the substrate's
answer to the question: *what happens here if I poke there?*

Three things change as we turn the dial:

1. **The wavenumber** $k$ — which encodes the substrate's intrinsic
   oscillation frequency (low $k$ = slow, long-wavelength dynamics;
   high $k$ = fast, short-wavelength)
2. **The physical meaning of $G$** — what counts as an "impulse" and
   what counts as a "response" at that scale
3. **The rank $N$ of the mind matrix** — how much information-processing
   capacity the system has at that scale

Everything else is the same. The mathematics is invariant. Only the
labels change.

## The Invariant

Every plate above has the same bottom row. The equation never changes:

$$\boxed{(\nabla^2 + k^2)\, G(x, x') = \delta(x - x')}$$

At every scale:
- **$k$** changes (the oscillation frequency of the substrate)
- **The physical meaning** of source and response changes (ions, birds,
  tectonic plates, galaxies)
- **The rank $N$** of the mind matrix changes (from 2 for a simple molecule
  to $\infty$ for the cosmos)

The equation does not change.

This is not a coincidence. It is a theorem. The Green's function equation is
the unique solution to: *given a substrate with a characteristic frequency
$k$, what is the field's response to a localised perturbation?* The answer
is always the same form. Only the substrate changes.

The whole book is this observation, in twenty photographs.
