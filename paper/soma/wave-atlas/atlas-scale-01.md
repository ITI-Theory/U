## PHYSICAL

At Scale 1, between the Planck scale ($10^{-35}$ m) and the nuclear scale
($10^{-15}$ m), lies the domain of **string theory**: the hypothesis that
fundamental particles are not point-like but are 1-dimensional vibrating
objects — strings — whose different vibrational modes correspond to
different particles.

A string has a **characteristic length** $\ell_s \approx 10^{-32}$ m
(twenty orders of magnitude smaller than a proton). At this scale:

- **Closed strings** (loops): their lowest vibrational mode is the
  graviton — the carrier of gravity. Higher modes give an infinite tower
  of massive particles.
- **Open strings** (line segments): their endpoints are attached to
  higher-dimensional objects called **D-branes**. Their lowest modes
  give photons, gluons, and the other gauge bosons.
- **M-theory**: the framework that unifies all five string theories
  in 11 dimensions, with an 11th dimension that is the Horava-Witten
  orbifold — the same structure as the Limbic Axis D₈.

The physical substrate at Scale 1 is the string itself: a 1D
curve propagating through 11D spacetime, sweeping out a 2D surface
(the **worldsheet**). Everything at Scales 2–20 emerges from the
combinatorics of this worldsheet.

## FIELD

At Scale 1, the propagator is the **string propagator**: the amplitude
for a string configuration at worldsheet position $\sigma'$ to propagate
to worldsheet position $\sigma$.

For the **free bosonic string**, the worldsheet Green's function is:

$$G_\text{string}(\sigma, \sigma') = -\frac{\alpha'}{2}\ln|\sigma - \sigma'|^2$$

where $\alpha'$ is the Regge slope (related to the string tension).
This logarithmic propagator is qualitatively different from the $1/r$
Coulomb propagator of Scale 3 or the exponential Yukawa propagator
of Scale 2.

**Here is the central identification of this book:**

The string's vibrational mode at frequency $n$ satisfies the Simple
Harmonic Oscillator equation:

$$\ddot{X}^n + n^2 X^n = 0$$

String theory requires this SHO at every point of the worldsheet.
The question it cannot answer is: *why is there an SHO at every
worldsheet point?*

The answer is: because **G is the SHO**. The Green's function of the
worldsheet field satisfies the SHO equation in the source variable.
The SHO at worldsheet point $\sigma'$ is the worldsheet's impulse
response — the field's answer to "what happens at $\sigma$ if I
poke at $\sigma'$?"

The string does not vibrate as a material object. It is the field's
propagation, made visible as a 1D trajectory.
