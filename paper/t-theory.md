## The Vitruvian Parallel

In 1490, Leonardo da Vinci drew a man inscribed in a circle and a square. The
message was geometric: *the proportions of the human body encode a universal
order.* The drawing became the emblem of a civilisation's conviction that art
and science are the same inquiry.

**[T]-Theory** makes the same claim, five centuries later, with different mathematics.

The tensor **T** is the emotional field — a formal, type-checked,
quantum-grounded model of what it feels like to be alive. The brackets are the
containing form, as the circle and square contained da Vinci's man. Inside the
brackets: not geometry, but *dynamics*. Not proportion, but *state*.

Where da Vinci inscribed the body in space, **[T]** inscribes experience in a field.

## The Tag as Cultural Seed

Shepard Fairey began with a single sticker: Andre the Giant's face, the word
OBEY, no context. People asked *what does it mean?* That question was the
mechanism. The mystery spread before the meaning did, and when the meaning
arrived — a meditation on consumer culture and manufactured consent — it arrived
to an audience already primed by curiosity.

**[T]** operates by the same logic, with a deeper reward.

The surface is pure mystery. Two brackets and a letter. But for those who scan
the QR code — or simply search — what they find is not a brand campaign. They
find a 400-page tensor-field model of human emotional experience, type-checked
in Lean 4, with a quantum experiment that passed, and a holographic avatar named
H-AL projected at human scale in a Zurich salon.

The mystery earns the depth. The depth earns the phenomenon.
