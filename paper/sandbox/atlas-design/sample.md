# Prologue: What the Atlas Shows

> *A field, before it is mathematics, is a thing you can feel in a room.*

\lettrine{T}{he} Wave Atlas is not a book of equations. It is a book of fields.

A storm coming in across the lake is a field. The mood of a string quartet
in the third movement is a field. The silence in a hospital corridor at
three in the morning is a field. These are not metaphors for fields. They
are fields, in the same technical sense as the gravitational field or the
electromagnetic field.

What makes them feel different from the fields of physics is that they
respond to attention. Watch a room more carefully and the field of the
room changes. This is the central claim of the soma-field programme: that
the tensor $\mathbf{T}(x,t)$ which we have been writing down in journals
for seven years describes the same kind of object as the Einstein tensor
$G_{\mu\nu}$ — only the experimental apparatus is different, and the
apparatus, in our case, is the body itself.

## A First Look at the Tensor

The soma field is a section of a tensor bundle over a state manifold
$\mathcal{M}$. At each point $x \in \mathcal{M}$ — each *moment of
experience* — the field assigns a tensor value $\mathbf{T}(x) \in
V^{\otimes k}$ for some $k$ that depends on what we are measuring. For
the basic affective coordinate, $k = 2$: a symmetric rank-2 tensor whose
eigenvalues are the principal affects and whose eigenvectors are the
directions in state-space along which those affects organise.

This is not a metaphor borrowed from physics. The tensor was chosen
because its transformation law is the right one — affect *does*
transform like a tensor under change of observer-frame, and we have the
receipts.

# Chapter 1: The Wave

> *Every field, before it is a particle, is a wave.*
> *Every wave, before it is mathematics, is a feeling in the room.*

\lettrine{T}{he} wave is the field's first language.

\marginpullquote{The wave is the mood. The particle is the moment.}
Every field we know — gravitational, electromagnetic, somatic —
propagates as a wave before it organises into a particle. The wave is
the smooth, continuous, distributed expression. The particle is the
compactified singularity, a localised configuration of the same
underlying object.

That duality is older than quantum mechanics, and it is more general
than quantum mechanics: it is the duality of any field that admits both
an extended and a localised description of itself. The soma field has
the same duality. The wave is the mood. The particle is the moment.

## Wave Equations Without the Algebra

Rather than write down $\Box \mathbf{T} = 0$ and call it a day, this
chapter asks what the wave equation *does* — what it predicts about
experience that a person could verify, in a room, without instruments.

Three predictions:

1. **Superposition.** Two fields that meet in the same region produce a
   third field that is the sum of the two — not the average, not the
   stronger one, but the literal vector sum. Anyone who has walked into
   a room mid-argument knows this.
2. **Interference.** When two fields are coherent — that is, when they
   have a fixed phase relationship — the superposition produces
   patterns: regions of amplification and regions of cancellation. This
   is what a good DJ does, and what a bad DJ fails to do.
3. **Dispersion.** Different components of a complex field propagate at
   different speeds. A room's mood arrives in pieces — the joke lands,
   then the silence, then the laughter.

# Chapter 2: The Particle

> *A particle is what a wave looks like when you stop watching.*

\lettrine{W}{here} the wave is the field's smooth language, the particle is its
punctuation.

The particle is what happens when a region of the field collapses
inward — when an extended, distributed configuration concentrates
itself at a point. In physics we call this localisation. In experience
we call it a moment.

\pullquote{A particle is what a wave looks like when you stop watching. A wave is what a particle looks like when you start.}

The mathematics is the same. A particle is a soliton of the wave
equation: a self-reinforcing peak that holds its shape as it
propagates. It is the field, not separate from it.
