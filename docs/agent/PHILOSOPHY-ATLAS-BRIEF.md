# Philosophy, Atlas, and App Brief

## Objective

Develop one coherent public explanation across three forms:

1. a philosophy book about what [T]-Theory claims and how claims are tested;
2. a visual Atlas that makes the scale/field model inspectable;
3. app panels that expose the same level-specific information interactively.

The Atlas is not a decorative companion. It is a flattened, printable view of the same curated model that the app exposes interactively.

## Philosophy Book Scope

The philosophy book should cover:

- philosophy of science: explanation, models, evidence, prediction, falsification, and underdetermination;
- abductive inference: how structural hypotheses are proposed, attacked, revised, and bounded;
- ontology: physical description, phenomenology, neutral-monist interpretation, and the limits of each claim;
- AI-assisted research: what an AI system can retrieve, compare, simulate, or criticize, versus what it cannot establish;
- formal verification: what a Lean kernel establishes and what it does not establish about premises or empirical reality;
- semantic interoperability: a small OWL/OpenCyc-facing ontology bridge for concepts such as proposition, evidence, observation, hypothesis, model, prediction, and contradiction.

Sherlock belongs here as an adversarial epistemic method, not merely as a software feature.

## Atlas/App Shared Model

Propose a compact, versioned level registry. Each level or scale should be able to provide:

- identifier, name, scale range, and time scale where relevant;
- field state and primary propagator/operator;
- reader-facing explanation;
- claim-status labels from `THEORY-STATUS.md`;
- linked app visual state, controls, and original screenshots;
- linked Atlas page/figure and source references.

The app may present this through contextual panels, tabs, or expandable information boxes. The Atlas may present the same record as a page, figure caption, comparison table, or annotated screenshot.

## Physics Comparison Material

Open educational physics texts may be used to identify canonical teaching topics and gaps, such as spacetime curvature, fields, propagators, compactification, or phase transitions.

Do not reproduce their prose, diagrams, or page layouts. Produce original explanatory text and original figures that state exactly what is standard physics, what [T]-Theory changes or adds, and what remains hypothetical.

## First Deliverable

Produce a read-only proposal, not code, containing:

1. philosophy-book outline and candidate source selection;
2. shared registry schema at the conceptual level;
3. app panel/screenshot plan;
4. Atlas chapter and original-figure plan;
5. minimal OWL/OpenCyc-to-Lean bridge boundary;
6. claim-status and evidence-risk review;
7. smallest safe implementation sequence.

Do not begin a book, app refactor, ontology import, or Lean formalization until this proposal is accepted.
