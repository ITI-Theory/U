# The Green Propagator

**G-ID:** *The Universal Propagator - $G(x,x')$ at the Hubble scale*

The Green Propagator answers the most fundamental question in physics: if
something happens here, what is felt there? In this Gateway book, it is the
invisible thread connecting every chapter, from the vibration of a guitar string
to the emotional state of a person in crisis to the expansion of the observable
universe. You do not need the mathematics to feel it. Look for the moments when
the book shows two very different things behaving in exactly the same way: that
sameness is the propagator at work. The whole programme has one equation; this
book shows you what it means.

There is a question that serious scientists rarely ask aloud: why does anything
feel like anything? The standard scientific picture can describe firing neurons,
circulating hormones, and evolutionary pressures, but has little to say about
experience itself: the raw fact that there is something it is like to be here.

The Universal Somatic Field is the [T]-Theory answer. It is proposed as a
physical tensor field, like a field of gravitation or electromagnetism, whose
internal structure describes the changing shape of sensation, emotion, and
awareness. The claim is mathematical rather than metaphorical: compactifying
M-theory along a specific geometric pathway yields a field equation with
attractor dynamics, and those attractors provide the model's account of
emotional states.

The chapters that follow work at three connected layers. First comes the
theory: the field equation, its tensor structure, and its energy landscape.
Second comes evidence: predictions about state transitions, trauma wells,
coupling, and measurable correlates. Third come implications for medicine,
physics, and ordinary life. The Gateway is the opening volume of a fifteen-book
fractal programme: the same field problem is taken up in the languages of
physics, neuroscience, clinical practice, music, law, economics, and cosmology.

This is the gateway. Follow the logic, retain scepticism where the evidence
requires it, and look for the same propagator at work across different scales.

# Conclusion: The Field Is the Territory

The book began with the apparent mismatch between a universe of matter and
forces and the fact of lived experience. The Universal Somatic Field addresses
that mismatch by placing experience inside the physical picture: not as a late
addition, but as a field with energy, dynamics, and measurable couplings.

Its three load-bearing claims are ontological, formal, and empirical. The
ontological claim is that the somatic field is physical in the same sense as an
electromagnetic field. The formal claim is that the field equation is derived
from M-theory compactification and that its attractor structure models an
emotional landscape. The empirical claim is that the framework produces
predictions that may succeed or fail under experiment. Each claim remains
bounded by the papers and proofs that support it.

What remains is substantial. Direct field measurement requires instruments that
do not yet exist; biological coupling constants need experiment rather than
order-of-magnitude estimates; and cosmological calculations require greater
control over compactification geometry. These are research programmes, not
reasons to blur the boundary between a derivation, a measurement, and an open
hypothesis.

The invitation is therefore modest and serious: read the evidence, test the
predictions, and decide what the field account explains. The equations are
written down, the proofs are checked, and the experiments have begun.
