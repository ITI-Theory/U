# Atoms and the Quantum Scale

Atomic physics is the first place in this book where everyday pictures fail quickly. Electrons in atoms are not tiny planets following classical orbits. They are described by wavefunctions whose squared magnitudes give probability densities, and only certain energy values are allowed. The chapter keeps that standard baseline clear before introducing the Atlas reading of atomic response as a level in a larger registry.

## 3.1 Quantization from boundary conditions

The simplest quantum bound states arise because waves must fit boundary conditions. A string fixed at both ends supports only wavelengths that fit an integer number of half-waves. An electron bound by the Coulomb potential is not a string, but the lesson carries over: allowed states are constrained by the operator, potential, and normalization condition. The stationary equation is $\hat H\psi=E\psi$, where $\hat H$ is the Hamiltonian operator and $E$ is an allowed energy.

Hydrogen remains a central teaching example because one proton and one electron make the calculation unusually clean. The exact quantum solution gives energy levels $E_n=-13.6\,\mathrm{eV}/n^2$ for principal quantum number $n$. Real atoms require spin, relativity, many-electron interactions, and quantum electrodynamics for high precision, but the baseline already shows how fields, operators, and spectra fit together.

::: {.example title="Example 3.1 A Lyman-alpha wavelength"}
For hydrogen, $E_1=-13.6\,\mathrm{eV}$ and $E_2=-13.6/4=-3.4\,\mathrm{eV}$. A transition from $n=2$ to $n=1$ releases $10.2\,\mathrm{eV}$. Using $hc\approx1240\,\mathrm{eV\,nm}$, the photon wavelength is $1240/10.2=121.6\,\mathrm{nm}$, in the ultraviolet.
:::

::: {.check-your-learning}
The $n=3$ hydrogen level has energy $-13.6/9=-1.51\,\mathrm{eV}$. A drop from $n=3$ to $n=2$ releases about $1.89\,\mathrm{eV}$.
:::

## 3.2 Measurement and scale

Quantum measurement does not mean that consciousness is inserted into physics. In textbook practice, measurement means an interaction that correlates a quantum system with an apparatus or environment strongly enough to produce a record. The record may be a detector click, a spectral line, or a track in a chamber. Interpretation remains a live philosophical and physical topic, but calculations of spectra and transition probabilities do not require a psychological field.

Scale also changes what can be ignored. At atomic length, $a_0=0.0529\,\mathrm{nm}$ sets the hydrogen radius scale. In a molecule, nuclei and electrons interact across several such lengths. In a cell, billions of molecules produce concentrations, membrane potentials, and reaction networks that are better treated statistically. A zoom from atoms to cells therefore loses phase information but gains variables that can be measured in living tissue.

## 3.3 The Atlas reading

For atoms, the Differences Ledger gives many baseline equations class E, meaning the [T]-Theory entry is identical in the appropriate limit. The ordinary Schrödinger equation remains the baseline. The Atlas contribution is the placement of atomic response inside a 31-level map and the comparison with higher-scale response grammar. That placement is `interpretive` unless a later model derives a new atomic prediction.

The phrase quantum scale should therefore be handled with restraint. QUANT-EXP-1, discussed in chapter 5, is an exact 8-qubit statevector simulation of an attractor model. It does not show that atomic electrons feel trauma, nor that a quantum computer was used. It shows that a particular mathematical model can be simulated and compared with a classical cold baseline.

::: {.key-terms}
**Wavefunction:** complex-valued quantum state representation. **Hamiltonian:** operator associated with total energy. **Energy level:** allowed stationary energy. **Probability density:** squared magnitude of a normalized wavefunction. **Spectrum:** set of emitted or absorbed frequencies.
:::

::: {.key-equations}
$\hat H\psi=E\psi$ is the stationary Schrödinger equation. Hydrogen has $E_n=-13.6\,\mathrm{eV}/n^2$. Photon energy satisfies $E=hc/\lambda$.
:::
