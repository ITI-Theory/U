# Cosmology and the Dark Sectors

Cosmology studies the universe as a dynamical spacetime filled with matter, radiation, and structure. Its claims are constrained by expansion, the cosmic microwave background, nucleosynthesis, supernovae, lensing, and galaxy surveys. This final chapter teaches the Friedmann baseline before presenting the [T]-Theory dark-energy and dark-matter numbers with their evidence labels and falsifiers.

::: {.learning-objectives}
By the end of this chapter, readers should be able to write the first Friedmann equation, convert the Hubble constant into SI units, compute the model value $\Lambda_{USF}$, compare $\Omega_{DM}=3/11$ with a Planck baseline, and distinguish derived-under-assumptions numbers from independent confirmation.
:::

## 10.1 Friedmann baseline

For a homogeneous and isotropic universe, the first Friedmann equation can be written as $H^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}$. The terms describe expansion rate, density, spatial curvature, and cosmological constant. Modern observations indicate a universe close to spatially flat with ordinary matter, dark matter, radiation, neutrinos, and dark energy.

The Hubble constant is often quoted in $\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$. Converting it matters because $\Lambda$ has units of inverse square length. With $H_0=67.4\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$, the SI value is about $2.184\times10^{-18}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. A dimensional mistake would change the cosmological constant by enormous factors.

::: {.example title="Example 10.1 Computing the USF Lambda value"}
The proposed formula is $\Lambda_{USF}=(21/11)H_0^2/c^2$. Using $H_0=2.184\times10^{-18}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ and $c=2.998\times10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ gives $\Lambda_{USF}=1.01\times10^{-52}\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}$. Against a Planck-scale baseline near $1.09\times10^{-52}\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}$, the value is about seven percent low.
:::

::: {.check-your-learning}
Because $\Lambda_{USF}$ is proportional to $H_0^2$, a one percent increase in $H_0$ produces about a two percent increase in the derived $\Lambda$ value.
:::

## 10.2 Dark matter fraction

The dark-matter proposal gives $\Omega_{DM}=3/11=0.2727$. A representative Planck 2018 value for cold dark matter is about $0.2645$, so the model number is roughly $3.1\%$ high relative to that baseline. The arithmetic is simple; the interpretation is not. Agreement at a few percent is not confirmation unless the assumptions are independently justified and competing models are addressed.

Dark matter is constrained by many observations. A successful theory must fit galaxy rotation curves, cluster lensing, the cosmic microwave background, baryon acoustic oscillations, structure formation, and non-gravitational searches or limits. The [T]-Theory statement is `derived-under-assumptions` because it follows from a compactification and localization model inside the programme. It is not an empirical detection of a particle or a proof against particle dark matter.

::: {.making-connections}
Open science textbooks usually present the dark sectors as problems with multiple evidence streams. This edition adds a model-derived overlay only after the standard evidence is on the page.
:::

## 10.3 Labels and falsifiers

The Differences Ledger classifies $\Lambda_{USF}$ and $\Omega_{DM}=3/11$ as class A new numbers with `derived-under-assumptions` evidence. That label means the numbers can be checked for algebra and compared with data, but their assumptions remain open. A future observation could improve agreement, worsen it, or reveal a non-gravitational dark-matter interaction that the model does not allow.

Falsifiers should be written beside the numbers. For dark energy, the programme must address redshift dependence, curvature, and independent $H_0$ constraints. For dark matter, it must face self-interaction limits, direct-detection results, lensing maps, and structure growth. If a confirmed non-gravitational dark-matter particle is found at the required cosmological abundance, a purely spatial-vacuum account would fail or need revision.

::: {.key-terms}
**Friedmann equation:** expansion equation for homogeneous cosmology. **Hubble constant:** present expansion rate. **Cosmological constant:** constant term with units $\mathrm{m^{-2}}$. **Density parameter:** density contribution divided by critical density. **Falsifier:** observation or calculation that would count against a claim.
:::

::: {.key-equations}
$H^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}$. The programme formula is $\Lambda_{USF}=(21/11)H_0^2/c^2$. The proposed dark-matter fraction is $\Omega_{DM}=3/11$.
:::

Cosmology has a strong observational baseline. The [T]-Theory dark-sector numbers are explicit, checkable, and potentially falsifiable, but they remain derived under assumptions rather than independent confirmation.
