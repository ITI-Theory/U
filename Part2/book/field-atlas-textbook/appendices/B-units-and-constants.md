# Appendix B: Units and Constants

The textbook uses SI units unless a chapter states a dimensionless ratio. The most common quantities are length $m$, time $s$, frequency $Hz$, energy $J$, field amplitude in model-specific units, and probability as a dimensionless number between 0 and 1.

| Quantity | Symbol | Typical unit | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speed of light | $c$ | $m\,s^{-1}$ | Used in cosmological expressions. |
| Planck constant | $\hbar$ | $J\,s$ | Appears in quantum equations. |
| Diffusion coefficient | $D$ | $m^2\,s^{-1}$ | Scale-dependent transport parameter. |
| Hubble parameter | $H_0$ | $s^{-1}$ | Cosmology baseline quantity. |

Unit conversion is part of the evidence discipline. A cross-scale diagram that cannot say what its units mean should be read as interpretive artwork rather than as a quantitative model.
