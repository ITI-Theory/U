# Glossary

**Action potential.** A travelling electrical pulse along a nerve or muscle membrane, produced by voltage-gated ion channels. It is a fast biological wave on an excitable substrate.

**Activator-inhibitor system.** A reaction-diffusion model in which one component amplifies a local pattern while another suppresses it, often at a different diffusion rate. Such systems can generate spots, stripes, and other morphogenetic patterns.

**ADE classification.** The list of simply-laced Lie types $A$, $D$, and $E$. In this programme it is used in the mathematical discussion of singularities and exceptional symmetry, not as a direct empirical claim.

**AdS/CFT correspondence.** A holographic duality between a gravitational theory in anti-de Sitter space and a conformal field theory on its boundary. It is a sourced precedent for boundary/volume equivalence, not a claim that the atlas systems are literally AdS/CFT systems.

**ALE space.** An asymptotically locally Euclidean space used in the study of certain singularities and their resolutions. It appears in the mathematical background to compactification models.

**Attractor.** A region or set in phase space toward which trajectories tend to move. Fixed points, cycles, and more complex sets can all be attractors.

**Autonomic nervous system.** The part of the nervous system regulating visceral functions such as heart rate, digestion, and arousal. Sympathetic and parasympathetic branches are central to organism-level regulation.

**Baroreflex.** A feedback loop that senses blood-pressure changes and adjusts heart rate and vascular tone. It contributes to low-frequency cardiovascular oscillations.

**Boundary condition.** A constraint placed at the edge of a domain or at an initial time. The same differential equation can give different patterns when the boundary conditions change.

**Calabi-Yau manifold.** A compact complex manifold used in many string-theory compactifications. It is part of the high-energy physics background, not a directly observed atlas object.

**Cardiac coherence.** A regular, narrow-band pattern in heart-rate variability, often near the baroreflex rhythm. It is a physiological descriptor, not by itself a therapeutic proof.

**Cartan subalgebra.** A maximal commuting subalgebra of a Lie algebra. Its dimension is the rank of the algebra.

**Coherence.** Stable phase relation among components of a wave or oscillatory system. Quantum, optical, neural, and social uses of the word require different measurements.

**Compactification.** The modelling step in which extra dimensions are curled or otherwise made small enough that an effective lower-dimensional theory is observed. Its details determine the resulting fields and symmetries.

**Coupled oscillator.** An oscillator whose motion influences, and is influenced by, another oscillator. Coupled oscillators can synchronise, phase-lock, drift, or become chaotic.

**Dark energy.** The name for the component driving the observed accelerating expansion of the universe. In standard cosmology it is well modelled by a cosmological constant, though its physical origin remains open.

**Dark matter.** Matter inferred from gravity but not directly detected electromagnetically. The programme's numerical comparisons are model-dependent and do not identify dark matter in nature.

**Density wave.** A pattern in which density maxima move or persist differently from the individual material elements. Spiral arms in disc galaxies are a standard example.

**Diffusion.** The smoothing of concentration or probability differences by random motion. Its simplest continuum description uses a diffusion constant with units of length squared per time.

**$E_8$.** The largest exceptional simple Lie group, with rank 8 and dimension 248. Any mapping from $E_8$ structure to affective modes is interpretive unless separately formalised and tested.

**Evidence badge.** A reader-facing label for claim status. FORMAL, SOURCED, and INTERPRETIVE correspond to formal proof, sourced or measured support, and model reading or open hypothesis.

**Field.** A quantity assigned to points of space, time, a body, or an abstract domain. Fields may be scalar, vector, tensor, spinor, or model-specific objects.

**Fold catastrophe.** A simple bifurcation in which a stable and unstable equilibrium meet and disappear. It is a candidate geometry for sudden transitions in some state-space models.

**$G_2$.** An exceptional Lie group that can be the holonomy group of seven-dimensional manifolds. It is important in M-theory compactification and in the programme's interpretive geometry.

**Green's function.** The response of a linear system to a point impulse. Once known, it can be used to build the response to more complicated sources by superposition.

**Hamiltonian.** A function representing the energy of a system and, in many settings, generating its time evolution. In stochastic models it is often paired with dissipation and noise.

**Holography.** The idea that information in a volume may be encoded on a boundary. In the atlas it is used carefully as a physical principle in its home contexts and as an analogy elsewhere.

**Hopfield network.** A recurrent neural network with an energy landscape and attractor states. The soma-field formalism is described as a tensor-valued generalisation under stated assumptions.

**HRV.** Heart-rate variability, the beat-to-beat variation in cardiac intervals. It is a non-invasive physiological signal useful for studying autonomic regulation.

**Kaluza-Klein mode.** A massive effective state arising from motion or fields in compact extra dimensions. Its mass scale depends on the size and geometry of the compact dimension.

**Langevin equation.** A stochastic differential equation combining deterministic drift with random noise. It is a natural language for noisy relaxation in an energy landscape.

**Lean 4.** A programming language and theorem prover used to check formal statements. Lean verifies derivations inside the formal system; it does not by itself validate empirical interpretation.

**Length scale.** The characteristic size at which a level is viewed. It tells the reader what has become visible and what has been averaged away.

**M-theory.** An eleven-dimensional theoretical framework unifying several string-theory limits. Its use in this programme is assumption-bound and interpretive unless a statement is separately proved.

**Mode.** A characteristic pattern of oscillation or response. Modes may be standing waves, eigenvectors, attractor directions, or named model states depending on context.

**Morphogen.** A chemical signal whose spatial concentration helps determine biological pattern formation. Morphogens are often modelled with reaction-diffusion equations.

**Orbital resonance.** A commensurate relation between orbital periods or frequencies. Resonance can stabilise, destabilise, or structure planetary and satellite systems.

**Phase space.** The space of possible states of a dynamical system. A trajectory in phase space records how the system changes over time.

**Propagator.** An operator or kernel that carries a state, disturbance, or probability amplitude from one place or time to another. Green's functions are common propagators.

**Quantum decoherence.** The loss of usable quantum phase relation through entanglement with an environment. Decoherence is central to assessing quantum claims in warm biological systems.

**QUANT-EXP-1.** An exact 8-qubit statevector simulation comparing barrier reachability under modelled classical and quantum annealing schedules. It is not a device run.

**Reaction-diffusion.** A system combining local reactions with spatial diffusion. It is a standard mechanism for pattern formation in chemistry and biology.

**Registry.** The shared data source that names levels, scales, equations, sectors, sources, and app renderers. It keeps the atlas and the Soma Machine aligned.

**Renormalisation.** A method for describing how effective parameters change with scale. In the atlas it is a disciplined way to ask what survives a zoom step.

**Respiratory sinus arrhythmia.** The heart-rate increase during inhalation and decrease during exhalation. It is mainly mediated by vagal control.

**Schumann resonance.** Standing electromagnetic modes of the Earth-ionosphere cavity, with a fundamental near 7.83 Hz. They are planetary electromagnetic phenomena, not evidence for human affective states by themselves.

**Soma field.** The programme's model object for coupled bodily, neural, and regulatory dynamics. It is a formal and interpretive construct whose clinical implications require testing.

**Standing wave.** A wave pattern with stationary nodes and antinodes. It arises when waves interfere in a bounded or resonant domain.

**Substrate.** The physical or model medium that carries a field, wave, or state variable. Changing substrate usually changes what can be measured.

**Tensegrity.** A structural principle in which compression elements are stabilised by a continuous tension network. Biological tensegrity is a hypothesis about connective-tissue mechanics.

**Threshold.** A value at which a system changes regime, such as firing, reporting, or leaving a basin. Thresholds require operational definitions before they become measurements.

**Turing pattern.** A spatial pattern produced by reaction and diffusion when a uniform state becomes unstable. The term comes from Alan Turing's morphogenesis work.

**Tunnelling.** Quantum passage through a classically forbidden barrier. In the atlas it must not be used as a metaphor for clinical change without an explicit interpretive label.

**Vagus nerve.** The tenth cranial nerve, carrying major parasympathetic and visceral afferent pathways. It is central to autonomic regulation and interoception.

**Wave equation.** A differential equation describing disturbances that propagate through a medium or field. Its solutions include travelling waves, standing waves, and resonant modes.

**Yukawa potential.** A short-range potential with exponential decay, originally used to model nuclear forces. It contrasts with the long-range Coulomb potential.
