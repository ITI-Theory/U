title: Chemistry to Biology
levels: [molecular, cellular-synaptic, local-circuit, whole-brain-cemi]

Sector II follows the scale band where chemical structure becomes living signalling. At the molecular level, the relevant physics is quantum and electromagnetic: nuclei and electron clouds form bonds, proteins fold, ligands bind, and conformational changes alter function. At the cellular-synaptic level, those molecular events are enclosed by membranes and converted into bioelectric variables: voltage, ion gradients, calcium, receptor state, vesicle release, and gene-regulatory feedback. At the local-circuit level, many excitable cells become a mesoscopic network with recurrence, inhibition, delay, oscillation, and phase. At the whole-brain-CEMI level, distributed source currents form measurable electric and magnetic fields, while the stronger claim that such fields integrate conscious content remains a hypothesis [@mcfadden2002a; @mcfadden2002b].

The ordinary science is therefore a sequence of controlled losses. Orbitals and bond vibrations matter for molecules, but a circuit cannot track every orbital. Ion-channel kinetics matter for cells, but a whole-brain field cannot track every channel. Each zoom preserves what still changes the next scale: thresholds, coupling strengths, timing, source currents, boundary conditions, phase relations, and response kernels. The compact equations used across the sector are not interchangeable proofs. Schrödinger form belongs to molecular state; the cable equation belongs to membrane voltage; a damped wave equation is a circuit-scale approximation; and a Helmholtz-style Green function is a way to display brain-scale source response. They are scale-specific ways of asking how a source produces a response, not claims that molecules, synapses, circuits, and consciousness are already the same physical object.

The historical thread is one of increasing access to hidden variables. Chemistry made bonding and molecular shape experimentally tractable. Electrophysiology then showed that nerves use ionic gradients rather than an unspecified vital force: Hodgkin and Huxley turned the spike into a conductance model, and Rall connected dendritic anatomy to cable theory [@hodgkin1952quantitative; @rall1962theory]. Synaptic physiology connected transmission to vesicles and receptors; microscopy and molecular biology added structural detail. At the circuit level, recordings from cortical neurons and population models showed how excitation and inhibition can make receptive fields, waves, and oscillations [@hubel1959receptive; @wilson1972excitatory]. At the whole-brain level, EEG and MEG made aggregate fields visible, but also exposed the inverse problem: external sensors see a projection, not a direct map of every source [@berger1929elektrenkephalogramm; @cohen1968magnetoencephalography; @nolte2004identifying].

The main open questions sit exactly at the boundaries. Developmental reaction--diffusion patterns show that biology can make form from local rules [@turing1952morphogenesis], and electrophysiology shows how membranes compute with ions. But it remains unsettled how dendrites, glia, extracellular fields, metabolism, and neuromodulation should be combined in a single predictive account of tissue. It also remains unsettled how much endogenous electromagnetic coupling contributes to cognition, how far CEMI theories can be tested against competing global-workspace or integrated-information accounts, and whether microtubules have any cognitive quantum role beyond ordinary cell biology. Hameroff--Penrose Orch OR is therefore kept only as an `open-hypothesis`, with decoherence objections explicitly visible [@tegmark2000importance; @hameroff2014consciousness]. Ordinary measurements of brain fields are `empirical-result`; the claim that those fields are the integrating medium of consciousness is `open-hypothesis`.

The carrying idea for the sector is response grammar `interpretive`. A perturbation enters a substrate; the substrate filters, delays, amplifies, damps, synchronises, or thresholds it; the next scale sees the surviving response, not the full microscopic history `interpretive`. In [T]-Theory this cross-scale grammar is `interpretive`, with some formal statements `derived-under-assumptions` inside *The Universal Somatic Field: Green's Functions as Scale-Invariant Oscillators across Twenty Scale Levels*. The safe use of the grammar is comparative: it asks which variables survive projection from chemistry to membrane, from membrane to circuit, and from circuit to whole-brain field. No level in this sector is claimed to prove consciousness, therapy, or feeling in isolated matter `open-hypothesis`. A decisive future test would show that the response variables retained by the grammar predict cross-scale behaviour better than established alternatives; a negative test would show that standard biochemical, electrophysiological, and network variables already explain the data without the extra interpretive layer.

For the atlas, this sector is also the first place where the word field risks becoming ambiguous. Molecular fields, membrane voltage, local field potentials, and whole-brain electromagnetic fields are all real scientific terms, but they do not carry the same evidence burden. The text therefore keeps the ordinary field first, names the instrument or equation that measures it, and only then introduces the [T]-Theory reading with labels. That discipline lets the lens-off account remain usable even where the lens-on hypothesis remains open.
