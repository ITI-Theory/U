Sector III begins after the bounded organism. Its first object is not a larger body but a relation: the dyad. From there the atlas moves to small groups, animal swarms, the bird--flock--roost side-path, cities, institutions, civilisational systems, and species-scale or stellar-neighbourhood comparison points. The ordinary sciences are heterogeneous: interaction studies, synchronisation theory, crowd science, active matter, ethology, urban studies, institutional analysis, historical sociology, evolutionary thinking, and some deliberately hybrid registry levels. The sector's discipline is therefore not one master explanation. It is careful accounting of which variables survive each zoom and which new variables must be added.

The dyad keeps two organismal boundaries while making coupling measurable. Gaze, speech timing, gesture, posture, music, movement, and physiology can be compared as paired time series, but common external pacing can imitate mutual influence [@tschacher2014nonverbal; @acebron2005kuramoto]. The path labelled **Organism to Dyadic Care** uses this narrow bridge: one body becomes two bodies in contact, with coupling and repair but no fusion. The next plate, **Small Group / Crowd / Assembly**, adds membership, density, shared source, role, norm, and procedure. Families, teams, audiences, choirs, bands, congregations, crowds, and assemblies can synchronise, deliberate, split, queue, cheer, sing, or coordinate work. Each participant remains a separate person; the group propagates and coordinates but does not feel as one subject `interpretive`.

The path labelled **Performance Ensemble / Audience** makes the group problem especially concrete. A performer, duo, ensemble, and audience share pulse, room acoustics, cueing, attention, expectation, and applause. Music psychology already treats entrainment, expectancy, contagion, and memory as distinct mechanisms rather than one cause [@juslin2013; @large1999dynamics]. In the programme, those mechanisms become candidate parameter operations in a response field `open-hypothesis`. The same path then zooms to the city that hosts the event: venues, rehearsal rooms, transit, schools, festivals, archives, media, markets, and licensing. The live pulse is not denied; it is integrated into a wider civic propagator.

Animal swarms are the sector's first major abstraction away from human meaning. Flocks, schools, herds, insect groups, drones, and software agents can show alignment, cohesion, separation, turning waves, and collective choice without central command [@vicsek1995novel; @couzin2002collective; @bialek2012statistical]. The registry edge from human group to animal swarm therefore states what is shared and what is not. Collective coordination is shared. Language, law, ritual, norm, legitimacy, and institutional memory are not shared by default `interpretive`. The path labelled **Engineered Agent Network** uses the clean coordination side of this distinction, moving from swarms of agents to urban infrastructures where precomputed response can sometimes reduce message passing `derived-under-assumptions`.

The bird side-path--bird, flock, colony or roost--is included to keep the atlas honest. A bird is a living aerodynamic body, not a human metaphor. A flock is active matter with neighbour alignment and air constraints. A colony or roost adds site recurrence, density, habitat, and transport. The side-path demonstrates how one organism-scale node can be followed through flight coordination into a durable collective place. It also prevents a human-centred reading of all collectives: the response grammar can compare propagation, but it may not import human affect into birds or roosts `open-hypothesis`.

The higher half of the sector widens from event and motion to durable collective media. Society/city levels add streets, buildings, utilities, platforms, markets, media, class boundaries, and public spaces. Regional/institutional levels add jurisdictions, laws, forms, archives, professions, budgets, and routines. The path labelled **Assembly to Institution** names this progression: from persons through pairs, groups, congregations, and cities to institutions, cultures, and civilisations. Institutions carry and constrain; they do not feel. They function as memory systems and boundary conditions, lowering some coordination costs while preserving exclusions or brittle rules `interpretive` [@north1990institutions; @batty2013new].

Civilisational/solar and species/stellar are hybrid transition levels. They compare persistence under long constraint without claiming that culture is gravity, species are stars, or history is orbital mechanics `interpretive`. A civilisation can store records, standards, energy systems, and transport networks across centuries. A species stores lineage, selection, drift, and reproductive isolation across generations. Stellar-neighbourhood readings belong to a different physical branch. The atlas keeps these labels because the Soma Machine shares a registry across book and app, but the prose must keep the branch boundaries visible.

The sector's open questions are empirical and ethical. When does a group model outperform individual or dyadic models? When is synchrony mutual influence, and when is it coercion, common source, or artefact? When can active-matter equations illuminate a crowd, and when do they erase meaning or power? Which institutional variables should enter a propagator, and who is harmed if the model optimises coordination without legitimacy? Sector III carries one idea upward: a bounded source, declared state space, and measured response can be projected to a larger scale only when lost variables, new variables, instruments, baselines, and failure conditions are named `derived-under-assumptions`.
