title: Individual to Collective

Sector III covers the move from a whole brain to a whole organism, then from one organism to two, and finally to many. Its ordinary science is deliberately heterogeneous. Brain-scale CEMI discussion begins with neural currents, synchrony, and measured electromagnetic fields, while remaining cautious about causal consciousness claims [@mcfadden2002a; @mcfadden2002b]. The human/vertebrate level adds interoception, fascia, posture, heart and respiratory timing, endocrine and autonomic regulation, movement, and environmental forcing [@craig2003; @ingber1997; @grossman2007toward]. Dyads introduce synchronisation and interaction timing, for which coupled-oscillator and phase-locking models are useful but limited [@acebron2005kuramoto; @tschacher2014nonverbal]. Animal swarms then replace pair labels with density, alignment, velocity fields, noise, and boundary dynamics [@vicsek1995novel; @toner1995long; @bialek2012statistical].

The sector's central plate is the human level. In the programme it is where affect is modelled as a field, where a Hopfield-style attractor landscape becomes the grammar for eight labelled organism states, and where music, rhythm, posture, memory, breath, and touch can enter as forcing terms `interpretive`. *The Missing Limbic Layer: A Somatic Field Extension of Hopfield Networks via the Correspondence Principle* makes this a modelling centre, not a clinical fact or proof of consciousness `derived-under-assumptions`. *The Soma-Field: A Wave-Based Model of Emotional Dynamics* and *The Physical Substrate of the Soma-Field* motivate the organismal substrate while keeping substrate identity open `open-hypothesis`. QUANT-EXP-1 remains an exact 8-qubit statevector simulation of reachability in a toy attractor landscape, not a hardware result, therapy result, or consciousness demonstration `simulated`.

The main physics that carries through the sector is response. A source perturbs a bounded system; the system answers through a kernel, damping term, attractor, phase-locking condition, or velocity field `interpretive`. What changes at each zoom is the variable that makes the response visible. CEMI needs fields over neural tissue. The human plate needs an organism state vector with noise, damping, forcing, and a landscape. The dyad needs off-diagonal delay because one organism's output becomes another organism's input. The swarm needs coarse-grained alignment, density, velocity, and boundary variables because named pairs are no longer enough to describe a group turn or escape wave `derived-under-assumptions`.

The same move also changes what must be protected. At the human level, the boundary is the biological organism: no aura, diagnosis, or therapeutic outcome is smuggled into the picture `open-hypothesis`. At the dyad level, there are two boundaries: the useful object is coupling, not fusion `interpretive`. At the swarm level, individual animals remain discrete even when the display uses a smooth field `interpretive`. The sector therefore works only if its reductions are explicit. It may average away molecular detail, private history, or individual trajectories for a particular display, but it may not pretend that those details never existed.

The ordinary open questions are correspondingly strict. Which electromagnetic variables are causal rather than epiphenomenal? Which interoceptive and biomechanical variables are necessary for organism-scale modelling? Which eight-mode coordinates can be operationalised? When does dyadic synchrony describe mutual influence, and when is it only a common response to the same stimulus? How far can active-matter equations travel before species biology, sensory range, terrain, or fluid medium breaks the analogy? These are empirical and modelling questions, not rhetorical ones.

The bird side-path--bird, flock, colony-roost--is included as a concrete named solution. It shows how one avian body becomes an active flock and how repeated flock movements become a site-level continuity pattern. The side-path is important because it removes the temptation to make every field human. A bird is governed by wings, air, gravity, muscle, and sensory control; a flock by neighbour alignment and aerodynamic constraints; a roost by density, recurrence, habitat, and transport. The response grammar can be compared across these levels `interpretive`, but human affect is not imported into birds, flocks, colonies, or roosts `open-hypothesis`.

Sector III therefore acts as a hinge in the Atlas. Below it, the emphasis is on material fields, organs, and neural activity. Above it, the emphasis shifts to many-agent, ecological, and eventually cultural patterns. Its one carrying idea is not that all scales are the same. It is that a bounded source, a declared state space, and a measured response can be re-expressed at a higher scale only after the lost variables, new variables, instruments, sampling limits, background drivers, comparison baselines, and model failure conditions have been named `derived-under-assumptions`.
