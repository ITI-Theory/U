This sector begins with rock bodies and ends with the dense remnants of stars. Its path label is **Geological to Stellar**, and the sequence is deliberately physical: geological, planetary, orbital-system, stellar, compact-object. The ordinary sciences change at every step. Geology studies faults, folds, basins, nappes, volcanic arcs, metamorphic histories, stress fields, and seismic waves. Planetary science couples those materials to whole-body gravity, rotation, oceans, atmospheres, mantles, magnetospheres, tides, radiation, and climate feedbacks. Orbital dynamics then reduces many of those bodies to masses, multipoles, drag terms, tidal responses, and trajectories. Stellar physics opens a plasma interior with hydrostatic balance, fusion, opacity, radiation, convection, magnetic activity, and oscillation modes. Compact-object physics finally compresses stellar history into white dwarfs, neutron stars, black holes, pulsars, accretion flows, jets, and ringdown.

The main continuity is not substance but response. Rocks store stress and release it as rupture, creep, seismic waves, or preserved structure [@aki2002quantitative; @turcotte2014geodynamics]. Planets filter impulses through layered media: mantle convection, ocean circulation, atmospheric waves, magnetospheric coupling, impacts, and radiative balance [@vallis2017atmospheric; @ipcc2021ar6wg1]. Orbital systems carry perturbations as changes of phase, eccentricity, inclination, resonance, spin, or tide [@murray1999solar]. Stars return forcing as spectra, luminosity changes, winds, flares, neutrinos, and oscillation modes [@aerts2010asteroseismology; @prialnik2009stellar]. Compact objects return sparse but powerful signals: pulsar timing, X-ray variability, accretion signatures, tidal deformability, lensing, shadow-scale emission, and gravitational-wave ringdown [@shapiro1983black; @abbott2016gw; @eht2019m87].

The open questions are correspondingly specific. Earth science still asks how earthquakes nucleate, how fluids alter faults, how mantle rheology changes with scale, and how climate feedbacks interact with geology. Planetary science asks which processes generalise from Earth to Mars, Venus, gas giants, icy moons, and exoplanets. Orbital science asks how migration, resonance, collision, small-body activity, rings, and comet tails preserve formation history. Stellar science asks how angular momentum, magnetism, convection, and mass loss reshape stellar evolution. Compact-object science asks what matter does at nuclear density, how jets launch, how black-hole information should be understood, and which ringdown deviations would signal new physics.

The evidence gradient is also part of the sector. Geological claims may be checked by field mapping, radiometric dates, seismic inversions, geodesy, laboratory rheology, and boreholes. Planetary claims combine those methods with spacecraft imaging, spectroscopy, gravity tracking, magnetometers, landers, ocean floats, weather stations, and climate models. Orbital claims are tested by astrometry, Doppler tracking, transits, occultations, radar ranging, and spacecraft navigation. Stellar claims use spectra, photometry, neutrinos, seismology, and population comparison. Compact-object claims are inferred from timing, gravitational waves, X-rays, gamma rays, radio jets, lensing, and horizon-scale interferometry. The Atlas should preserve this instrumental plurality rather than flattening every level into one visual metaphor. `empirical-result`: The strongest statements in the sector are ordinary measurements. `interpretive`: The [T]-Theory layer is a secondary way to mark how those measurements can be read as source, medium, response, and boundary.

`interpretive`: The [T]-Theory idea carried through the sector is a conservative response grammar. Each level names a substrate, an impulse, a kernel or governing equation, a returned signal, and a coarse-graining boundary. The Atlas can compare geological stress memory, planetary delay, orbital resonance, stellar oscillation, and compact-object ringdown only when their variables remain explicit. `derived-under-assumptions`: *The Universal Somatic Field: Green's Functions as Scale-Invariant Oscillators across Twenty Scale Levels* provides the catalogue framework, but the ordinary science at each level remains the primary content [@johnson2026usf].

What is integrated out changes sharply. A named fault becomes lithospheric strength or topography at planetary scale. A complex planet becomes an effective gravitating body at orbital scale. An orbital environment becomes composition, spin, accretion, or tidal forcing inside a star. A luminous star becomes mass, spin, remnant type, field, and collapse history at compact-object scale. These losses are not errors. They are what make a higher-level variable useful.

The negative boundary should be visible in both book and app. This sector does not claim feelings in rocks, planets, or stars; it does not infer intention from orbits; it does not treat black holes as minds. `open-hypothesis`: Any stronger cross-scale identification would need declared variables, predictions, and failure conditions. Lens off, the pictures should show ordinary geology, planetary layers, orbital diagrams, stellar interiors, and compact-object environments. Lens on, the overlay should add response waves, kernels, delays, damping, resonances, and integration boundaries, never personification.
