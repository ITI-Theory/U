This sector runs from bound orbits to the observable universe. It begins with systems whose members can be drawn as discrete bodies: planets around a star, stars in clusters, compact remnants, discs, haloes, and clusters. It ends with structures that are measured statistically and causally: filaments, the cosmic web, and the observable universe as a past-light-cone domain. The ordinary science is the physics of gravitating systems, but not one equation repeated without change. Newtonian inverse-square gravity works for many orbits and clusters; stellar structure requires hydrostatic balance, fusion, opacity, and convection; compact objects require general relativity and dense-matter physics; galaxies require collisionless dynamics, gas, feedback, and dark haloes; cosmology requires expansion, perturbations, surveys, and causal horizons.

The main continuity is source and response. Mass shapes potentials. Stress-energy perturbs geometry. Pressure and magnetic fields redirect plasma. Expansion damps perturbations. Radiation carries information with delays. Surveys reconstruct structure from signals that left their sources at different times. At small astronomical scales, the eye can be fooled into thinking the map is simultaneous. At cosmic scale it cannot be: redshift, look-back time, selection functions, and model assumptions become part of the object being described.

The sector's ordinary open questions are not decorative. How do protoplanetary and debris systems evolve under migration, resonance, and collision? How do stars transport angular momentum and magnetic energy? What is the equation of state inside neutron stars, and how should black-hole information be understood? How do discs maintain spiral and bar structure? What is the particle or field nature of dark matter? Why does the expansion history fit a dark-energy component so well, and how should current tensions be resolved? How do feedback, gas physics, and selection effects alter the inferred cosmic web? Planck 2018 supplies the baseline cosmological values used in the atlas: $\Omega_\Lambda=0.6847$, $\Omega_c=0.2645$, and $H_0=67.36$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$ [@planck2018cosmology].

The object catalogue in this band should also remain disciplined. Nebulae and remnants belong to ordinary gas, dust, shock, and radiation physics. Accretion discs and relativistic jets belong to compact-object environments. Comets, rings, and belts belong to orbital dynamics plus small-body material physics. At larger scale, clusters, filaments, walls, voids, baryon acoustic oscillations, the cosmic microwave background, and the observable horizon are not mystical entities; they are measured or inferred structures with stated equations, instruments, and uncertainties.

The same discipline applies to pictures. A renderer can show a glowing halo, a filament, or a causal shell, but the caption must say whether the feature is directly imaged, statistically inferred, simulated, or interpretive. At these scales the most honest image is often layered: luminous tracers in one layer, mass inference in another, gas pressure in another, and model comparison badges kept separate from measurements. That separation protects both the science and the programme reading.

The [T]-Theory idea that carries through the sector is response grammar `interpretive`. Each level asks what counts as substrate, what counts as field, what impulse can be applied or inferred, what response is carried upward, and what detail is integrated out `interpretive`. *The Universal Somatic Field: Green's Functions as Scale-Invariant Oscillators across Twenty Scale Levels* supplies the scale catalogue under declared assumptions `derived-under-assumptions` [@johnson2026usf]. The cosmology papers add model-dependent comparisons: $7/11$ for the leading dark-energy fraction in *The Cosmological Constant as the Vacuum Amplitude of the Universal Somatic Field*, and $3/11$ for dark matter in *Dark Matter as the Spatial Vacuum of the Universal Somatic Field: Ω_DM = 3/11* `derived-under-assumptions` [@johnson2026cosconst; @johnson2026darkmatter]. These are comparisons beside Planck 2018 values, not measurements of nature `derived-under-assumptions`.

The negative boundary matters as much as the positive one. This sector does not claim feelings, intention, agency, or proved consciousness in comets, stars, galaxies, clusters, filaments, the web, or the universe `open-hypothesis`. It does not turn gravity into psychology. The lens-on view is an overlay that marks response, coarse-graining, and analogy; the lens-off view remains complete ordinary science. Where the programme offers a formal or model-derived claim, the label must say so. Where it offers only a reading, the atlas should leave it as a reading `interpretive`.
