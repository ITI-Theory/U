This sector begins after the single-star and compact-object material treated in Sector IV. Its path label is **Galactic to Cosmic**, and its registry sequence is stellar-cluster, galactic-disc, galactic-halo, galaxy-cluster, cosmic-filaments, observable-universe, cosmic-web. The ordinary science is still gravitational, but the objects are no longer one star or one remnant. They are populations, rotating sheets, inferred haloes, bound multi-galaxy systems, filamentary bridges, causal volumes, and web topology.

The first step is the move to ensemble variables. A stellar cluster preserves common origin, membership, age, metallicity, velocity distribution, relaxation, evaporation, and tidal stripping [@portegies2010starclusters; @gaia2018dr2]. A galactic disc then averages many stellar populations into a rotating sheet of stars, gas, dust, magnetic fields, nebulae, bars, spiral arms, and feedback, governed by Poisson gravity, hydrodynamics, star formation, and dark-halo coupling [@binney2008galactic; @rubin1980rotation]. A galactic halo extends the question into a wider inferred mass environment: streams, satellites, lensing shear, circumgalactic gas, and rotation curves return signals from a distribution whose dark component is measured gravitationally, not directly.

Cluster scale adds another kind of separation. A galaxy cluster contains many galaxies and haloes, hot intracluster plasma, dark matter, diffuse light, shocks, and lensing mass. In mergers, collisionless galaxies and dark matter can respond differently from collisional gas, so one event produces several observable channels [@sarazin1986xray; @clowe2006bullet]. Cosmic filaments then make clusters into nodes or boundary conditions inside larger bridges, sheets, and voids. Filaments are not solid tubes; they are statistically defined overdensities inferred from galaxy positions, weak lensing, gas signals, absorption systems, and simulations [@bond1996cosmicweb; @tempel2014filaments].

The last two entries share the same nominal $10^{26}$ m scale but not the same display. Observable-universe scale names the causal horizon volume: the region from which signals can reach the observer in an expanding universe. Cosmic-web scale names the organised large-scale structure within that horizon: nodes, filaments, walls, voids, BAO features, survey masks, tracer bias, and retarded light-cone mapping. The step is therefore topological and observational rather than a claim of greater size.

The sector's open questions are the central questions of modern large-scale astrophysics and cosmology. How do clusters form and dissolve? How do discs maintain bars and spirals while feedback changes the gas? What is the particle or field nature of dark matter? How much baryonic matter sits in haloes and filaments? How do selection effects and tracer bias alter web maps? What causes accelerated expansion, and how should current $H_0$ and clustering-amplitude tensions be resolved? Planck 2018 supplies the baseline values used here: $\Omega_\Lambda=0.6847$, $\Omega_c=0.2645$, and $H_0=67.36$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$ [@planck2018cosmology].

The observational discipline of the sector is layered. A cluster can be dated by its colour-magnitude diagram, but a disc requires gas maps, rotation curves, and stellar-population synthesis. A halo is inferred from streams, satellites, lensing, and gas absorption rather than from a visible surface. A galaxy cluster requires optical redshifts, X-ray plasma, microwave pressure, radio emission, and gravitational lensing to be put in one account. Filaments and the web require catalogues, algorithms, correlation functions, power spectra, and simulations, with survey masks and tracer bias declared. Observable-universe claims add CMB likelihoods, supernova calibration, BAO rulers, weak lensing, and cosmic variance. `empirical-result`: The lens-off atlas must show these as measurements and model fits with uncertainty, not as cosmic iconography.

`interpretive`: The [T]-Theory idea carried through Sector V is response grammar under increasingly severe coarse-graining. A perturbation becomes a phase-space tail in a cluster, a density wave in a disc, a stream or lensing shear in a halo, a shock or pressure map in a cluster of galaxies, a filament skeleton in the web, or a statistical feature in a horizon-limited survey. `derived-under-assumptions`: The programme's $7/11$ dark-energy and $3/11$ dark-matter numbers are model-dependent comparisons from the cosmology papers, to be placed beside Planck values and never described as empirical derivations of nature [@johnson2026cosconst; @johnson2026darkmatter; @planck2018cosmology].

The negative boundary remains essential. Sector V does not claim feelings, intention, agency, or proved consciousness in clusters, galaxies, haloes, filaments, the observable universe, or the cosmic web `open-hypothesis`. It does not turn gravity into psychology. Lens off, the pictures should show stellar populations, discs, haloes, clusters, filaments, horizons, survey slices, and web topology with ordinary uncertainty. Lens on, the overlay may mark response channels, kernels, damping, causal limits, and coarse-graining boundaries, but it must keep measurements, simulations, model-derived comparisons, and interpretation visibly separate.
