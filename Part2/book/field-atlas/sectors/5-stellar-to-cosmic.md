This sector runs from bound orbits to the observable universe. Its ordinary science is the physics of gravitating systems: inverse-distance potentials, plasma modes, strong-gravity ringdown, many-body stellar dynamics, rotating galactic discs, dark haloes, clusters, filaments, the cosmic web, and the light-cone boundary of observation. Across the band, the key operation is not a single universal equation but a family of source-response descriptions: mass shapes potentials, stress-energy perturbs geometry, pressure supports plasma, expansion damps perturbations, and surveys reconstruct structure from delayed signals.

The main open questions are also ordinary scientific questions. How exactly do discs maintain or renew spiral structure? What is the microphysics of neutron-star matter and black-hole information? What is dark matter? Why does the measured expansion history fit a dark-energy component so well, and how should current tensions be resolved? How do feedback, gas physics, and selection effects alter the inferred cosmic web? Planck 2018 gives the baseline values used here: $\Omega_\Lambda=0.6847$, $\Omega_c=0.2645$, and $H_0=67.36$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$ [@planck2018cosmology].
