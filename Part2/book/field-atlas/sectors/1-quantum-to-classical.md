Sector I covers the smallest band in the atlas: from the Planck boundary through string/M-theory model language, nuclear matter, atoms, and molecules. It is called "Quantum to Classical" because it follows the first route by which effective, named objects become usable. At the lower boundary there are no ordinary objects to draw. There are amplitudes, candidate histories, and the warning that smooth spacetime and quantum field theory on a fixed background have reached their shared limit. String and M-theory then offer a structured but unconfirmed language of worldsheets, branes, compact dimensions, dualities, and mode spectra. Nuclear physics is different again: it is experimentally dense, with hadrons, nuclei, binding energies, scattering, decay, and short-range strong response. Atomic physics brings in the Coulomb field, electron orbitals, spectroscopy, photons, clocks, and ions. Molecular physics turns those atomic ingredients into bonds, conformations, reaction coordinates, catalysts, crystals, and biochemical function.

The sector's ordinary science is therefore not a single settled theory stretched across five powers of description. It is a chain of effective descriptions. At the Planck boundary, the safest statement is amplitude and propagator bookkeeping. At the string boundary, the variables become worldsheet coordinates, compactification choices, and allowed modes under stated assumptions. At the nuclear level, the variables become quark, gluon, hadron, nucleon, nucleus, and scattering variables. At the atomic level, most nuclear detail is compressed into charge, isotope, spin, magnetic moment, and mass while electron states take over. At the molecular level, even many electron details are compressed into bonds, electron density, torsions, conformers, solvent effects, and energy surfaces.

The open questions change with the variables. Quantum gravity asks what the correct substrate is, whether spacetime is emergent, and whether any Planck-scale signature can be measured. String/M-theory asks how vacua are selected and whether distinctive low-energy evidence exists. Nuclear theory asks how confinement, many-body forces, neutron-rich matter, and the QCD phase diagram generate observed nuclei. Atomic and molecular science are far more directly tested, but still ask how precisely complex spectra, correlations, reactions, excited states, and macromolecular dynamics can be predicted.

The idea carried through the sector by [T]-Theory is deliberately modest: each level can be displayed as an impulse-response grammar--source, kernel, boundary condition, response `interpretive`. Where the kernel and variables are ordinary measured science, the atlas says so `empirical-result`. Where the programme reuses the same grammar across unlike substrates, the claim is a modelling architecture from *The Zoomable Universal Somatic Field: A Scale-Invariant Green's Function Architecture* `open-hypothesis` [@johnsonzsf2026]. The sector is valuable precisely because it prevents overreach. It shows that the same diagrammatic grammar can pass through very different evidence states: unmeasured quantum-gravity boundary, theoretical string boundary, measured nuclear response, precision atomic physics, and chemically measured molecular function.

No level in this sector is assigned feeling, intention, or consciousness `interpretive`. "Information" means amplitude, state, quantum number, spectrum, sequence, conformation, or constraint, depending on the ordinary substrate. "Memory" first becomes safe at the molecular level only in the physical-chemical sense that a conformation or sequence can persist and affect later reactions. The atlas will later compare such persistence with biological regulation and somatic dynamics, but this sector supplies the discipline: every upward move must state what survives, what is averaged away, and which evidence label applies.

The sector also sets the first boundary between equations and pictures. A lower-level equation may be exact only inside an approximation, while the picture is a renderer aid. The Coulomb kernel, Yukawa kernel, worldsheet propagator, and molecular Hamiltonian are not interchangeable. They are examples of how different substrates answer different disturbances. This is the point at which the atlas can be both readable and honest: the visual grammar repeats, but the physics does not collapse into one substance or one evidence category, and every caption should preserve that distinction in print and software.

Sector I also establishes the first renderer logic. Lens off should move from almost objectless mathematical boundary to increasingly concrete objects: worldsheets, nuclei, atoms, and molecules. Lens on should not add mystical content. It should add kernels, response contours, boundary conditions, state transitions, and energy surfaces. The result is a scale ladder that works without [T]-Theory, then becomes a labelled response atlas when the interpretive layer is switched on.
