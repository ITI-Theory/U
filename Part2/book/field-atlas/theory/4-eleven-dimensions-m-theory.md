# T4 — Eleven dimensions and M-theory {#theory-eleven-dimensions}

## 1. Dimensional bookkeeping

![Figure T4.1 — The programme's bookkeeping split M4 × P3 × L1 × C3 totals eleven dimensions but is not a physical compactification by itself.](figures/theory/T4_1_dimension_blocks.png){width=92%}

A dimension is an independent coordinate needed to specify position in a chosen space. Ordinary spacetime in general relativity is four-dimensional: three spatial coordinates plus one time coordinate. String theory and M-theory use additional dimensions because the consistency of their extended objects and supersymmetries demands more coordinates in the underlying mathematical theory. The textbook baseline is separate from the programme's dimensional budget; see Penrose 2004, chs. 31 and 32 for the standard string/M-theory entry points.

The programme uses the formal count

$$
M_4\times P_3\times L_1\times C_3,
$$

where $M_4$ denotes local spacetime, $P_3$ a spatial vacuum block used in the dark-matter derivation, $L_1$ a localisation or line factor, and $C_3$ a compact/vacuum block used in the dark-energy derivation. The count $4+3+1+3=11$ is bookkeeping inside P21/P22-style assumptions. It is not the same as deriving the observed universe from an M-theory compactification. [derived-under-assumptions]

This distinction prevents overclaim. Physical compactification begins with a higher-dimensional theory on a product or fibration, then derives lower-dimensional fields, spectra, moduli, and couplings. The programme's count instead organises response sectors and compares fractions such as $7/11$ and $3/11$ with cosmological data. The numerical comparison can be tested and criticised without claiming that a full string vacuum has been constructed. [derived-under-assumptions; open-hypothesis]

## 2. Kaluza-Klein circle: the mass tower

![Figure T4.2 — Kaluza-Klein momentum around a circle appears in four dimensions as a tower of masses n/R.](figures/theory/T4_2_kk_tower.png){width=92%}

The simplest compactification is a field on ordinary spacetime times a circle of radius $R$. Let $y\sim y+2\pi R$ be the circular coordinate and let a five-dimensional scalar field satisfy

$$
(\Box_4+\partial_y^2)\Phi(x,y)=0.
$$

Periodicity forces a Fourier series

$$
\Phi(x,y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\phi_n(x)e^{iny/R}.
$$

Substitution gives

$$
(\Box_4-n^2/R^2)\phi_n(x)=0.
$$

A four-dimensional observer sees a massless mode $n=0$ and massive modes with $m_n=|n|/R$ in units where $\hbar=c=1$. Small circles hide the tower at high energy; large circles would produce light extra states. [derived-under-assumptions]

This calculation is the cleanest way to see what compactification means. The extra coordinate is not merely ignored. Its allowed momentum becomes particle data in the lower-dimensional theory. More complicated compact spaces replace the integer $n^2/R^2$ by eigenvalues of a Laplacian or Dirac operator on the internal manifold. Geometry becomes spectrum. The programme's dimensional fractions should therefore not be mistaken for a Kaluza-Klein spectrum unless such an internal operator and eigenvalue problem are supplied. [interpretive]

## 3. Tori, Calabi-Yau spaces, and G₂ spaces

![Figure T4.3 — Compactification schematics: a circle, a torus with two cycles, and a labelled Calabi-Yau/G₂ cartoon.](figures/theory/T4_3_circle_torus.png){width=92%}

A two-torus has two independent cycles. A field on $T^2$ has two integer momenta and hence a tower labelled by $(n_1,n_2)$. The torus is flat, so its holonomy is trivial. More realistic string compactifications use curved spaces whose holonomy preserves some supersymmetry. Calabi-Yau threefolds have $SU(3)$ holonomy and are central in ten-dimensional superstring compactifications to four dimensions. Seven-dimensional manifolds with $G_2$ holonomy are central in compactifications of eleven-dimensional M-theory.

Holonomy describes how vectors or spinors rotate after parallel transport around closed loops. On a sphere, a vector transported around a triangle returns rotated. The set of all such possible rotations is the holonomy group. Special holonomy means this group is smaller than the generic rotation group, which can preserve covariantly constant spinors and hence supersymmetry. This is geometry doing physical work, not decoration. [derived-under-assumptions]

Calabi-Yau and $G_2$ spaces are not drawn accurately by the small cartoons used in textbooks or figures. Their useful properties are encoded in differential forms, cycles, singularities, moduli, and spectra. A sketch can show that some directions are compact and structured; it cannot prove a compactification. P24's $G_2$ discussion is therefore treated as a programme construction around an eight-mode matrix and a seven-direction fold, not as a completed M-theory vacuum. [derived-under-assumptions; open-hypothesis]

## 4. The duality web

![Figure T4.4 — Duality graph with M-theory at the centre, five ten-dimensional string theories, and eleven-dimensional supergravity limit.](figures/theory/T4_4_duality_web.png){width=92%}

The five perturbative ten-dimensional superstring theories are type-one strings, Type IIA, Type IIB, heterotic $SO(32)$, and heterotic $E_8\times E_8$. During the second superstring revolution these were understood as limits of a larger non-perturbative structure commonly called M-theory. Type IIA at strong coupling opens an eleventh circular dimension; heterotic $E_8\times E_8$ at strong coupling is related to an interval with gauge sectors at the boundaries. [derived-under-assumptions]

T-duality relates compactification on a circle of radius $R$ to one on a radius proportional to $\alpha'/R$, exchanging momentum and winding modes. S-duality relates strong and weak coupling in certain theories. The web does not mean the theories are visually similar; it means observables in one description can be mapped to observables in another under a precise transformation. This is the model for any serious cross-domain identity claim: a duality requires a dictionary, not a slogan.

M-theory itself is not fully defined by a single elementary Lagrangian in all backgrounds. Eleven-dimensional supergravity is its low-energy limit. Branes, compactification spaces, fluxes, singularities, and dualities make the subject technical. The programme may use M-theory language as an organising source only where it keeps this hierarchy: baseline physics, formal analogy, then labelled programme claim. [interpretive]

## 5. Holonomy by transport

![Figure T4.5 — Parallel transport around a loop can rotate a frame; special holonomy restricts the allowed rotations.](figures/theory/T4_5_holonomy_transport.png){width=92%}

A connection tells how to compare vectors at nearby points. Moving a vector around a loop and returning to the start may rotate it. The collection of all rotations obtained from all loops is the holonomy group. In $n$ dimensions the generic Riemannian holonomy lies in $SO(n)$. Special holonomy is a reduction to a proper subgroup such as $SU(3)$ in six dimensions or $G_2$ in seven.

For $G_2$ manifolds, a distinguished three-form $\varphi$ is preserved by parallel transport. The stabiliser of a generic positive three-form in seven dimensions is the exceptional group $G_2$. If such a torsion-free $G_2$ structure exists, the metric has special properties and the compactification can preserve minimal supersymmetry in four dimensions. This is a mathematical theorem about differential geometry. [derived-under-assumptions]

The programme's use of $G_2$ has a different status. It uses a seven-direction structure to interpret a reduced emotional/gauge sector and compares an empirical-style eight-mode coupling matrix with a geometric ideal. P24 reports $W_8=(6/5)I_8+\delta W$ with $\operatorname{tr}\delta W=0$ and $\|\delta W\|_F/\|W_8\|_F=0.484$. The trace statement is formal arithmetic in the defined matrix; the claim that this measures biological symmetry breaking is `derived-under-assumptions` and open to re-estimation. [kernel-verified where named; derived-under-assumptions]

## 6. The octonion Fano plane

![Figure T4.6 — Fano-plane mnemonic for octonion multiplication, a standard route into G₂ as the automorphism group of the octonions.](figures/theory/T4_6_fano_plane.png){width=92%}

The octonions extend the real, complex, and quaternionic number systems to eight real dimensions. They are non-associative but alternative. Their seven imaginary units can be arranged on the Fano plane, where each oriented line encodes a multiplication rule. The automorphism group preserving octonion multiplication is $G_2$. This supplies one standard bridge between seven-dimensional geometry and the exceptional group.

The Fano plane in the figure is a mnemonic, not a proof. A proof defines the octonion product and shows that its automorphisms form the compact real form of $G_2$. The visual value is pedagogical: it explains why a seven-object pattern often appears near $G_2$ language. P24's eight-to-seven discussion uses this neighbourhood, but the decisive programme object is the actual $W_8$ decomposition and any future data that estimate or falsify it. [derived-under-assumptions]

## 7. Three readings kept separate

![Figure T4.7 — Three readings: physical extra dimensions, programme dimensional budget, and BRECVEMA/gauge fold; arrows mark analogy rather than equality.](figures/theory/T4_7_compactification_three_readings.png){width=92%}

There are three distinct uses of compactification language in the surrounding work. The first is standard physics: extra dimensions are compact, and their geometry determines lower-dimensional spectra and interactions. The second is the P21/P22 sector budget: fractions of an eleven-part decomposition are compared with cosmological density parameters. The third is the P24 fold from an eight-mode matrix toward seven independent symmetry-breaking directions.

Keeping the readings separate avoids a false syllogism. It would be wrong to argue that because M-theory has eleven dimensions and the programme has an eleven-part count, the programme has derived M-theory. It would also be wrong to dismiss all comparisons because one reading is not a complete compactification. Each claim gets its own test: Kaluza-Klein spectra for physical compactification, Planck comparison and redshift behaviour for dark-sector fractions, and matrix/data re-estimation for the gauge fold. [derived-under-assumptions; open-hypothesis]

## 8. The eight-to-seven fold

![Figure T4.8 — P24 fold: W8ℝ decomposes into an isotropic part (6/5)I8 plus traceless δW, with seven traceless directions displayed.](figures/theory/T4_8_gauge_fold.png){width=92%}

The P24 object is an $8\times8$ real coupling matrix $W_8^{\mathbb R}$. The reported decomposition is

$$
W_8^{\mathbb R}=\frac65 I_8+\delta W,
\qquad \operatorname{tr}(\delta W)=0,
\qquad \frac{\|\delta W\|_F}{\|W_8\|_F}=0.484.
$$

The scalar part carries one isotropic direction. The traceless remainder carries deviations from that isotropic ideal. The phrase eight-to-seven fold is therefore best read as a decomposition into one mean direction plus seven independent contrast directions, not as a literal proof that a biological system lives on a $G_2$ manifold. [derived-under-assumptions]

A clear falsifier is available. Re-estimate $W_8$ from a defined dataset and recompute the trace split, row sums, eigenstructure, and Frobenius ratio. If the matrix is not close to the stated form, the P24 numerical claim changes or fails. If the form persists, the result still needs interpretation: persistence of a matrix pattern is not yet a physical compactification. [open-hypothesis]

## 9. Branes, moduli, and what compactification must deliver

String/M-theory compactifications contain more than a shape for hidden dimensions. Branes can wrap cycles; fluxes can thread internal directions; singularities can support gauge sectors; moduli describe continuous deformations of size and shape. A lower-dimensional model must explain why moduli are stabilised, which gauge groups appear, which matter representations survive, and what hierarchy of masses is produced. These are demanding constraints. [derived-under-assumptions]

A modulus illustrates the issue. If a compact circle has radius $R$ and no potential fixes $R$, the lower-dimensional theory contains a scalar field corresponding to changes in that radius. Observations severely constrain light scalars coupled to matter or gravity. Realistic compactification therefore needs a mechanism that gives moduli acceptable masses or screens their effects. A dimensional count alone cannot solve this problem.

The swampland programme sharpens the boundary further by asking which effective field theories can arise from quantum gravity and which belong to the inconsistent "swampland." Positive dark energy, de Sitter-like vacua, and scalar potentials are active topics of debate. Any programme that invokes M-theory while proposing a cosmological constant must therefore separate a numerical density comparison from the harder task of constructing an allowed vacuum. [open-hypothesis]

## 10. Local physical dimensions versus internal model axes

Physical dimensions, internal state dimensions, and display axes should not be merged. A phase space for a pendulum is two-dimensional even though the pendulum moves in ordinary space. An eight-component emotional-mode vector is an internal model state, not eight spacetime dimensions. A compactification coordinate is a physical or geometric coordinate in a higher-dimensional theory. The same numeral can occur in all three contexts with different meaning.

This distinction is important for the $4D/8D/11D$ language around the programme. The ordinary physics baseline is four-dimensional local spacetime. The eight-dimensional life or BRECVEMA layer is a state-space or coupling description. The eleven-part count is a sector bookkeeping device used in the dark-sector papers. None of these statements by itself constructs an eleven-dimensional physical universe with the desired particle spectrum. [interpretive]

A strong future result would specify a map from the bookkeeping sectors to a compact geometry, derive a low-energy spectrum, preserve local GR, and reproduce the dark-sector fractions without fitting them after the fact. Until then, the proper label for the bridge from M-theory compactification to the programme's dimensional grammar is `open-hypothesis`, while the arithmetic inside the grammar remains `derived-under-assumptions` or `kernel-verified where named`.

## 11. What the five string limits contribute

Each perturbative string limit carries different weak-coupling data. Type IIA contains oriented closed strings and non-chiral ten-dimensional supersymmetry; its strong-coupling limit is described by an extra circular dimension. Type IIB is chiral and has an important self-duality structure. Type-one string theory includes open and closed strings with an $SO(32)$ gauge sector. The two heterotic theories combine supersymmetric right-moving modes with bosonic left-moving structure, giving either $SO(32)$ or $E_8\times E_8$ gauge symmetry.

These distinctions matter because a duality web is not a decorative pentagon. A map between two corners preserves a specified set of physical quantities while changing the variables used to describe them. For example, T-duality exchanges momentum modes around a circle with winding modes around the dual circle. S-duality exchanges strong and weak coupling in settings where it applies. The visual graph is only a summary of these dictionaries. [derived-under-assumptions]

A future programme claim that uses this web should therefore identify which corner supplies the baseline, which limit is being invoked, and what observable survives the translation. A phrase such as "M-theory-like" is too weak for a technical claim. A declared low-energy limit, compact space, brane content, and response observable would be stronger. [open-hypothesis]
