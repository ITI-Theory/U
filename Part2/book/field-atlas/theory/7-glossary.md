# Glossary {#theory-glossary}

**Absorbing boundary.** Boundary condition or numerical layer intended to let outgoing waves leave a finite domain with minimal reflection.

**Boundary condition.** Extra information specifying how a field behaves at the edge of its domain; examples include fixed, free, Robin, periodic, and absorbing conditions.

**Calabi-Yau manifold.** Compact complex manifold with $SU(3)$ holonomy, widely used in superstring compactifications from ten to four dimensions.

**Coarse-graining.** Replacement of many microscopic variables by fewer effective variables at a chosen resolution.

**Compactification.** Use of compact extra dimensions so that a higher-dimensional theory appears lower-dimensional at accessible energies.

**Convolution.** Integral that sums delayed or displaced copies of a source weighted by a kernel.

**Coulomb kernel.** Long-range static Green function proportional to $1/r$ in three spatial dimensions.

**Dark energy.** Component inferred from accelerated cosmic expansion; in ΛCDM it is represented by a cosmological constant.

**Dark matter.** Gravitating component inferred from rotation curves, lensing, clusters, CMB structure, and large-scale formation, without confirmed non-gravitational detection.

**Derived-under-assumptions.** Evidence label for a result that follows from explicitly stated assumptions, definitions, axioms, or model choices.

**Dispersion relation.** Relation $\omega(k)$ between frequency and wavenumber for a wave component.

**Duality.** Precise map between two descriptions that preserves observables or dynamics under a stated dictionary.

**Effective variable.** Coarse variable, such as density or phase, used after microscopic details have been averaged or projected away.

**Eigenmode.** Nonzero pattern returned by an operator up to multiplication by an eigenvalue; in bounded wave problems it gives a normal mode.

**Field.** Assignment of a typed value to each point of a domain, such as scalar height, vector velocity, tensor curvature, or spinor amplitude.

**Friedmann equation.** General-relativistic equation relating cosmic expansion rate to density, curvature, and cosmological constant in homogeneous-isotropic models.

**G₂ holonomy.** Special seven-dimensional Riemannian holonomy preserving a positive three-form; important in M-theory compactification.

**Green function.** Impulse response of a linear operator with specified boundary conditions.

**Group velocity.** Velocity $d\omega/dk$ of a wave-packet envelope in a dispersive medium.

**Holonomy.** Rotation or transformation obtained by parallel-transporting objects around closed loops; the set of all such transformations forms the holonomy group.

**Interpretive.** Evidence label for a conceptual mapping or explanatory reading that does not by itself establish mechanism.

**Kaluza-Klein tower.** Sequence of lower-dimensional modes whose masses arise from quantised momentum or eigenvalues in compact extra dimensions.

**Kernel-verified.** Evidence label for a named Lean theorem that compiles in its proof surface under its definitions and axioms.

**LocalGR gate.** Programme constraint preserving standard local general relativity while testing global dark-sector reinterpretations.

**M-theory.** Non-perturbative eleven-dimensional framework whose limits include eleven-dimensional supergravity and the five ten-dimensional superstring theories.

**Mode.** Allowed pattern of oscillation or response selected by operator and boundary conditions.

**Open-hypothesis.** Evidence label for a proposed claim with a stated route to formal or empirical discrimination.

**Phase velocity.** Velocity $\omega/k$ of a constant-phase crest for a monochromatic wave component.

**Pole.** Complex frequency or momentum value where a transfer function or propagator denominator vanishes; pole position encodes stability and resonance.

**Projection.** Map from an internal model state to a measured or displayed observable.

**Propagator.** Kernel carrying source information to response; in quantum field theory it often appears as an inverse operator in momentum or spacetime variables.

**Quality factor.** Dimensionless measure $Q=\omega_0/(2\gamma)$ for a damped oscillator, indicating resonance sharpness and decay time.

**Retarded.** Causal choice of Green function with no response before the source event.

**Robin condition.** Boundary condition combining value and normal derivative, typically $\alpha u+\beta\partial_nu=0$.

**Simulated.** Evidence label for a claim produced by a computational model or numerical experiment.

**Source.** Term $J$ that drives a field equation or response model.

**Superposition.** Linear rule allowing sums of solutions to remain solutions.

**T-duality.** String-theoretic duality relating compactification radius $R$ to a reciprocal radius proportional to $\alpha'/R$, exchanging momentum and winding descriptions.

**Wave.** Time-dependent pattern in a field, often transporting phase, energy, momentum, or information without transporting the material substrate in the same way.

**Yukawa kernel.** Screened static Green function proportional to $e^{-mr}/r$, characteristic of a massive mediator.

**Zoom operator.** Programme map between level descriptions specifying which variables, kernels, boundaries, sources, and observables are preserved or retyped.

**Advanced Green function.** Inverse of a differential operator whose support lies before the source event; useful mathematically but not a classical causal response.

**Characteristic impedance.** Ratio controlling reflection and transmission at a boundary between wave media; for an ideal string it is $\sqrt{T\mu}$.

**Critical density.** Cosmological density $3H_0^2/(8\pi G)$ used to define density parameters $\Omega_i$.

**Dimensionless number.** Unit-free ratio, such as Reynolds number or quality factor, used to compare regimes across different scales.

**Fano plane.** Seven-point mnemonic for octonion multiplication; connected to $G_2$ through octonion automorphisms.

**Modulus.** Parameter describing a continuous deformation of compactification geometry, such as the radius of a circle.

**Poynting vector.** Electromagnetic energy-flux vector $\mu_0^{-1}\mathbf E\times\mathbf B$.

**Swampland.** Proposed set of effective field theories that appear consistent at low energy but cannot arise from a complete quantum-gravity theory.

**Transfer function.** Frequency-domain ratio between output and input for a linear time-invariant system, often written as the Fourier transform of an impulse response.
