title: "The Body: Where Feelings Are Felt"
---

Ask people where in the body they feel anger, fear, sadness or happiness, and they colour different maps, consistently across cultures: anger warms the head, chest, arms and hands; sadness cools the limbs; happiness warms almost everything [@nummenmaa2014bodily] `sourced`. The plates follow those self-reported maps qualitatively for anger, anxiety, grief, joy and calm. Three further states the explorer offers, freeze, hypervigilance and flow, are artistic placements with no such map behind them `interpretive`.

At 8D the body becomes signalling: nerves carry impulses between body and brain, and the vagus nerve links heart and gut to the brainstem [@porges2011]. At 11D the model draws one field that couples the whole body at once, its "second nervous system" `open-hypothesis`. The plate shows the drawing; the map underneath it is the measurement.

The body's hall is the heart's left atrium. Blood fresh from the lungs enters it through the four pulmonary veins, and with each beat the mitral (bicuspid) valve in its floor opens into the left ventricle, which sends the blood up the aorta [@openstax2022anatomy] `sourced`. In the plate the veins are the light, the valve the floor, and ridges like the pectinate muscles of the atrium's appendage line the walls; the proportions are for the eye `interpretive`. Its doorways lead on: the way in to the brain, the body's feelings, the bloodstream, the whole person and the ureter.
