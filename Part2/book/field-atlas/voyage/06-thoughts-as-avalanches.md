---
title: Thoughts as Avalanches
---

Activity in cortical tissue comes in bursts. Recorded in slices, the bursts have sizes that follow a power law: many small, a few large, with no typical size, the signature of a system at the edge of instability [@beggs2003neuronal; @bak1987self] `sourced`. The explorer draws thoughts this way. Each burst is a cascade of a branching process in which every active unit wakes each of two neighbours with probability $\sigma/2$; at $\sigma = 1$ the process is critical and the sizes follow $P(s) \propto s^{-3/2}$ `simulated`. The figure below is that rule run sixty thousand times: at $\sigma = 1$ the points follow the line, and a little below it, at $\sigma = 0.8$, the large bursts disappear.

A burst that spreads far enough becomes, in the picture, a thought that reaches awareness, the global-workspace reading of ignition `interpretive`. The conditions the programme models change the bursts: more of them with ADHD, fewer and more focused with autism, and echoes with C-PTSD, a burst coming back fainter as in a cathedral [@P13]. Those are the model's operators, not diagnoses `open-hypothesis`.
