title: "The Ureter: A Stone, in Four Stages"
---

The voyage ends with a barrier met in the body. A kidney stone blocks the ureter; urine backs up, the tube swells above it, and the smooth muscle squeezes harder and more often to push it on. Each wave that crashes into the stone is a spasm, so the pain comes in waves: colic [@openstax2022anatomy] `sourced`. The four plates follow the process as a patient lives it: the stone; a stent that holds the ureter open, with a mild, persistent signal while it is in; the laser that breaks the stone to dust; and the stent removed, the tube healing. Each plate shows the inside of the tube and the body from outside together `interpretive`.

Unlike the gates of the path, a stone is mechanical: calm or alarm does not open it. What the limbic field changes is the pain. Visceral pain travels as C-fibre signals to the spinal cord and on to the thalamus and the insula [@craig2003] `sourced`, and the last plate follows that route. With the alarm raised, the same stone hurts more `interpretive`. This is a teaching picture, not medical advice or a model of any patient.
