# I · The Rights Invariant

A legal right $R$ is a topological invariant of the social field if:
$$\oint_\gamma \nabla R \cdot d\mathbf{x} = n \in \mathbb{Z} \quad \forall\,\gamma$$

Non-zero winding number $n$ = the right is \textbf{topologically protected}.
It cannot be removed by smooth deformations of the legal field —
only by a phase transition (revolution, constitutional crisis).

This is the USF version of the argument from natural law.

# II · Precedent as Field Memory

The common law principle of \textit{stare decisis} $\equiv$ the legal system's
memory kernel:
$$G_\mathrm{precedent}(\tau) = G_0\,e^{-\tau/\tau_\mathrm{law}}$$

Older precedents decay exponentially; recent cases dominate.
$\tau_\mathrm{law}$ varies by jurisdiction: constitutional courts have long
memory ($\tau_\mathrm{law}$ = decades); administrative tribunals have short
memory ($\tau_\mathrm{law}$ = months).

# III · Constitutional Attractors

A constitutional order = a deep attractor basin in the space of
legal configurations. Features:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Stability}: high energy barrier to leave the basin
\item \textbf{Amendment procedure}: the basin-reshaping mechanism
\item \textbf{Constitutional moment}: Ackerman's theory = a phase transition
\item \textbf{Failed states}: field without stable attractor = constitutional breakdown
\end{itemize}

# IV · Mathematical Co-identification

$$\text{Legal precedent} \equiv G_\mathrm{law}(\tau) \equiv K_\mathrm{somatic}(\tau)$$

The mechanism of legal memory is structurally identical to somatic memory.
The same differential equation governs both. Only the substrate differs.
