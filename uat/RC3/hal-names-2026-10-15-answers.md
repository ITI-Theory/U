### 3. Cinema’s T-stop (Transmission Stop): Does It Help or Hinder?

In cinematography, a **T-stop** is the true photometric measurement of light actually transmitted through a lens to the sensor (accounting for glass absorption and reflectance: \\(T = f / \sqrt{\text{transmittance}}\\)), whereas an **f-stop** is merely a theoretical geometric ratio of focal length to entrance pupil.

#### Why It Helps
1. **Mathematical Isomorphism with Tunnelling:** In [T]-Theory, the transmission of energy across a potential barrier (such as escaping a freeze well in `LimbicTunnel.lean`) is governed by the quantum transmission coefficient:
   \\[T = e^{-W}\\]
   A "[T]-stop" as a measure of **actual energetic throughput across a threshold** rather than an idealized classical opening matches the cinema definition.
2. **The "T" Brand Line:** It reinforces the recursive [T] branding (Tensor, Threshold, Trauma, Transformation, Transmission).
3. **Calibrated Reality vs Fiction:** Just as a cinematographer uses T-stops because lenses lose light through multi-element glass, the Soma Machine’s core ethos is measuring what the body actually conducts (effective transmission) rather than theoretical checklists.

#### Why It Hinders
* **Semantic Ambiguity with "Tour Stops":** In the Soma Machine app, each scale and chapter is called a **stop** (e.g. "stop at the Limbic Hinge", "step out at the 11D floor") [14, 15]. Calling baseline settings a "[T]-stop" risks confusing a *camera exposure/transmission parameter* with a *waypoint in the atlas tour*. 

*Verdict:* It **helps significantly**, provided it is used strictly for the **lens/transmission calibration** (the baseline aperture that sets how much somatic disturbance gets through), while navigation waypoints remain simply **tour stops**.
