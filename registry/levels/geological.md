## In ordinary science

The geological level is the scale of rock bodies, faults, folds, basins, nappes, and tectonic plates. Its measurable variables include mineral composition, density, elastic moduli, temperature, stress, strain, fracture orientation, fault slip, uplift, erosion, seismic velocity, and earthquake waveforms. Lengths range from outcrop metres to mountain belts hundreds of kilometres wide. Times range from seconds for seismic waves to millions of years for folding, metamorphism, and plate motion.

The registry equation, $\rho\,\partial_{tt}u_i=C_{ijkl}\partial_j\partial_k u_l$, is the elastic wave equation in index form. In words, rock displacement $u_i$ accelerates according to density $\rho$ and the stiffness tensor $C_{ijkl}$, which tells how stress and strain couple in different directions. This equation underlies seismology: an earthquake or controlled source sends P-waves, S-waves, and surface waves through the Earth, and their arrival times and amplitudes reveal structure [@aki2002quantitative]. For slower deformation the same rocks may creep, fold, fracture, or flow according to temperature, pressure, fluids, and rheology [@turcotte2014geodynamics].

The Glarus thrust is a good scientific example when stripped of personal narrative. In eastern Switzerland, older rocks were transported over younger rocks along a major low-angle thrust fault, leaving a dramatic structural contact visible across the UNESCO Swiss Tectonic Arena Sardona [@unescoSardona]. It is not a mystical sign or a literal wave frozen in stone. It is evidence for large-scale Alpine shortening, nappe transport, and long-duration deformation. The visible line works pedagogically because it makes geological time legible in one outcrop-scale feature.

Geology therefore supplies one of the clearest ordinary cases of stored constraint. Rocks can hold stress for long periods; faults can release it suddenly; folded strata can preserve a record of forces no longer present. The evidence is cross-checked by mapping, geochronological dating, mineral textures, balanced cross-sections, GPS motion, laboratory rheology, and seismic data rather than by a single scenic exposure alone. Timescales are calibrated independently.

## The pictures

- **Lens off:** A mountain cross-section or outcrop-scale structural diagram: older unit above younger unit, thrust plane, folded beds, fault arrows, and a 100 m to 10 km scale bar. Add a small inset seismogram showing P, S, and surface-wave arrivals from a fault impulse.
- **Lens on:** Overlay the rock with a stress-response field. Bright bands follow the thrust and fold hinges; wavefront rings show seismic propagation from a slip patch. A transparent memory layer marks stored strain and past displacement without anthropomorphic or clinical labels attached.
