## In ordinary science

A dyad is two living organisms interacting while remaining two separate systems. The ordinary science is not a claim about shared mind; it is the measurement of coupling. In humans this may include gaze, turn-taking, posture, speech timing, musical metre, distance, facial expression, and autonomic rhythms. In other vertebrates it may include courtship, parent-offspring contact, dominance encounters, cooperative motion, and acoustic signalling. The important shift from the single organism is that each body becomes part of the other body's environment.

The registry equation, $|\omega_A-\omega_B|<\Delta\omega_{\mathrm{lock}}(\kappa)$, is a phase-locking condition. In words, two oscillators with natural rates $\omega_A$ and $\omega_B$ can lock if their detuning is smaller than the locking range produced by coupling strength $\kappa$. This is the same mathematical family as the Kuramoto model and other synchronisation systems: weakly coupled oscillators can settle into a stable relative phase rather than drift indefinitely [@kuramoto1984chemical; @acebron2005kuramoto; @strogatz2003sync]. The variables may be acoustic phase, movement timing, respiratory rhythm, gesture onset, or a deliberately simplified proxy.

Empirical dyad work measures synchrony rather than assumes it. Nonverbal synchrony studies compare motion, affect ratings, and interaction timing; results depend on task, relationship, measurement window, and analysis method [@tschacher2014nonverbal]. Music cognition gives a clean physical example because pulse, metre, and expectation can be represented as time-varying auditory forcing and listener prediction [@large1999dynamics; @juslin2013]. Cross-cultural rhythm studies also warn against reducing entrainment to a universal metronome; metre is embodied, learned, and socially organised [@clayton2000time].

The relevant scale is roughly body-to-room scale: centimetres for gesture, metres for spacing, milliseconds to seconds for timing, and minutes for an interaction episode. Because coupling can be mutual, asymmetric, helpful, stressful, intentional, or accidental, ordinary analysis must record task and context as well as timing. The upper-level variables are therefore not the full internal state of either organism. They are relational observables: delay, phase, mutual information, coupling strength, shared task constraints, and boundary conditions.

## The [T]-Theory reading

The dyad is the first level where an off-diagonal propagator is explicit: one organism's output enters another organism's field as delayed forcing `interpretive`. *Mathematical Co-identification: A Formal Model of Therapeutic Attunement* treats this as structural import, not identity between persons `derived-under-assumptions`. The dyadic Green's-function response $G_{AB}(t-\tau)$ keeps two histories and two boundaries while representing influence across the gap `derived-under-assumptions`. *Music-Induced Affect Dynamics: A Soma-Field Model of the BRECVEMA Mechanisms* supplies one example: sound becomes a carrier for timing, prediction, and affective forcing `interpretive`. The programme carries up the human level's attractor grammar but integrates out private state except where it appears as measured interaction `interpretive`. A dyad can therefore be drawn without narrating biography, diagnosis, or intimacy `interpretive`. Nothing here claims telepathy, merged consciousness, therapy, or a guaranteed regulatory benefit `open-hypothesis`.

## The pictures

- **Lens off:** Show two seated or standing bodies in the same room, with visible speech bubbles or sound waves, gaze lines, gesture arrows, and separate ECG or breathing traces. Keep the bodies distinct and include a two-metre scale bar.
- **Lens on:** Add a labelled delayed coupling kernel between the bodies. The overlay should show two separate state vectors, small phase clocks, and arrows marked $G_{AB}(t-\tau)$ and $G_{BA}(t-\tau)$, with partial phase-locking rather than fusion.
