## In ordinary science

A dyad is two living organisms interacting while remaining two separate systems. The ordinary science is not a claim about shared mind; it is the measurement of coupling. In humans this may include gaze, turn-taking, posture, speech timing, musical metre, distance, facial expression, and autonomic rhythms. In other vertebrates it may include courtship, parent-offspring contact, dominance encounters, cooperative motion, alarm signalling, and acoustic exchange. The important shift from the single organism is that each body becomes part of the other body's environment.

The registry equation, $|\omega_A-\omega_B|<\Delta\omega_{\mathrm{lock}}(\kappa)$, is a phase-locking condition. In words, two oscillators with natural rates $\omega_A$ and $\omega_B$ can lock if their detuning is smaller than the locking range produced by coupling strength $\kappa$. This is the same mathematical family as the Kuramoto model and other synchronisation systems: weakly coupled oscillators can settle into a stable relative phase rather than drift indefinitely [@kuramoto1984chemical; @acebron2005kuramoto; @strogatz2003sync]. The variables may be acoustic phase, movement timing, respiratory rhythm, gesture onset, call-response delay, or a deliberately simplified proxy. The equation does not say that all dyads should synchronise; it only gives one condition under which a pair of simplified oscillators can.

Empirical dyad work measures synchrony rather than assumes it. Nonverbal synchrony studies compare motion, ratings, interaction timing, and statistical coupling; results depend on task, relationship, measurement window, sampling rate, and analysis method [@tschacher2014nonverbal]. Music cognition gives a clean physical example because pulse, metre, and expectation can be represented as time-varying auditory forcing and listener prediction [@large1999dynamics; @juslin2013]. Cross-cultural rhythm studies also warn against reducing entrainment to a universal metronome; metre is embodied, learned, and socially organised [@clayton2000time].

The relevant scale is roughly body-to-room scale: centimetres for gesture, metres for spacing, milliseconds to seconds for timing, and minutes for an interaction episode. The energy is still metabolic and mechanical inside each organism, but the explanatory variables are relational. A spoken syllable, gaze shift, foot tap, wing display, or call can alter the other participant's sensory input. Because coupling can be mutual, asymmetric, helpful, aversive, intentional, accidental, strong, weak, or absent, ordinary analysis must record task and context as well as timing. The upper-level variables are therefore not the full internal state of either organism. They are relational observables: delay, phase, mutual information, coupling strength, shared task constraints, turn structure, spatial boundary, and noise.

Dyad science is also a warning against attractive pictures. A pair can show similar timing because of shared external pacing rather than mutual influence. Apparent synchrony can be produced by filtering, windowing, or common trends. Conversely, meaningful coordination can involve anti-phase timing, role differentiation, interruption, avoidance, or deliberate mismatch. The ordinary dyad is therefore not a moral or therapeutic unit. It is a two-body measurement problem in which each body remains bounded, historically distinct, and only partly observed.

## How it was found and measured

The dyad level grew from several traditions. Physics supplied the mathematics of synchronisation, including the classic clock examples discussed by Huygens and later dynamical-systems treatments [@strogatz2003sync]. Biology and ethology supplied careful observation of paired behaviour: courtship displays, parent-offspring exchange, territorial encounters, grooming, and signalling were studied as organised interactions rather than isolated acts [@tinbergen1963aims]. Psychology, linguistics, and music research then measured turn-taking, conversational timing, joint action, and entrainment with cameras, microphones, rating scales, motion capture, and physiological sensors.

Modern dyad studies often combine multiple data streams: audio envelopes, video keypoints, accelerometers, ECG or respiration, eye tracking, and event annotations. The analysis may use cross-correlation, recurrence quantification, phase extraction, coherence, Granger-style models, or coupled-oscillator fits. Each method imposes assumptions about time scale and direction. A dyad is therefore "found" only when the pair, task, channel, and coupling statistic are all named. The most reliable measurements keep the two histories separate while estimating how much one time series helps explain the other.

## Objects at this level

### Interaction pair

The basic object is an interaction pair: two bounded organisms sharing space, signals, and time. Classical accounts describe sensory exchange, motor response, attention, distance, and task constraints. The [T]-Theory reading treats the pair as two organismal fields coupled by off-diagonal response kernels `interpretive`. The boundary condition is essential: the model keeps two bodies and two histories, not one blended subject `open-hypothesis`.

### Conversation

A conversation is a timed exchange of speech, gesture, gaze, silence, and repair. Classical linguistics and psychology study turn-taking, prosody, semantics, pragmatics, and social context. In the programme, conversation is one carrier for delayed forcing between organismal states `interpretive`. The claim is structural: timing and content can perturb response trajectories. It is not a claim that speech guarantees regulation, agreement, or shared experience `open-hypothesis`.

### Musical duet

A musical duet is a pair coordination task with unusually explicit timing. Classical variables include tempo, phase, metre, auditory feedback, gesture, score, improvisation rule, and error correction. It is useful because coupling can be measured without pretending that the partners are identical. The [T]-Theory reading treats pulse and phrasing as controlled inputs and mutual predictions `derived-under-assumptions`, while any affective interpretation remains task-bounded `interpretive`.

### Animal pair

An animal pair may be a courting pair, parent and offspring, rivals, cooperative movers, or caller and responder. Classical ethology studies signalling, spacing, dominance, kinship, learning, and ecological context. The programme can reuse the dyad grammar for delay and coupling `interpretive`, but it must not import human intimacy, clinical vocabulary, or human affect categories into non-human pairs `open-hypothesis`.

## The [T]-Theory reading

The dyad is the first level where an off-diagonal propagator is explicit: one organism's output enters another organism's field as delayed forcing `interpretive`. *Mathematical Co-identification: A Formal Model of Therapeutic Attunement* treats this as structural import, not identity between persons `derived-under-assumptions`. The dyadic Green's-function response $G_{AB}(t-\tau)$ keeps two histories and two boundaries while representing influence across the gap `derived-under-assumptions`. The reverse kernel $G_{BA}(t-\tau)$ may be different, because coupling can be asymmetric `derived-under-assumptions`.

What survives from the human/vertebrate level is the grammar of forcing, damping, memory, and bounded response `interpretive`. What is integrated out is most private organism detail unless it appears in measured interaction. The dyad therefore carries only the variables needed for the relational display: timing, delay, coupling strength, role, channel, context, and boundary preservation `interpretive`. *Music-Induced Affect Dynamics: A Soma-Field Model of the BRECVEMA Mechanisms* supplies one example: sound becomes a carrier for timing, prediction, and affective forcing `interpretive`. The same form could describe gaze, gesture, speech, or animal signalling if the variables are specified `open-hypothesis`.

A dyad can therefore be drawn without narrating biography, diagnosis, intimacy, or therapeutic benefit `interpretive`. Nothing here claims telepathy, merged consciousness, clinical treatment, shared subjectivity, moral harmony, or guaranteed regulation `open-hypothesis`. The programme's discipline is to show two state vectors and a coupling kernel, not to collapse the two participants into one.

## Open questions and tests

Ordinary science still has unresolved dyad questions. Which coupling statistics survive changes of window length and filtering? How should common external drivers be separated from mutual influence? Which timing patterns matter for speech, music, parent-offspring interaction, courtship, or joint action? The [T]-Theory reading can be tested by comparing explicit off-diagonal kernel models with simpler baselines that use only shared stimuli or independent organism histories `open-hypothesis`. If $G_{AB}$ is meaningful, it should improve prediction of one participant's measured trajectory from the other's prior behaviour under controlled tasks `derived-under-assumptions`. Failure to outperform common-drive models would weaken the dyadic field reading for that case `open-hypothesis`.
