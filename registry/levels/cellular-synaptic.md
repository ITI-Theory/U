## In ordinary science

At the cellular and synaptic scale, biological information is carried by membranes, ion channels, cytoskeletal structures, organelles, neurotransmitter release, and local chemical gradients. A typical neuron soma is tens of micrometres across; dendritic spines, axon initial segments, and synaptic clefts are smaller, while action potentials last milliseconds and many synaptic currents decay over milliseconds to seconds. Excitable membranes maintain voltage differences by separating sodium, potassium, chloride, calcium, and other ions across lipid bilayers. Hodgkin and Huxley's squid-axon model showed how voltage-dependent sodium and potassium conductances can generate a travelling spike rather than a passive electrical leak [@hodgkin1952quantitative].

For a thin dendrite or axon segment the app uses the compact cable-equation form $(\partial_x^2-\lambda^{-2})V=I_{\mathrm{inject}}$. In words, the curvature of membrane voltage $V$ along the cable is balanced against passive leakage over a length constant $\lambda$ and an injected current source $I_{\mathrm{inject}}$. A fuller time-dependent cable equation includes membrane capacitance, axial resistance, membrane resistance, and a source current; its impulse response says how a brief input spreads, decays, and is delayed along the process. Rall's cable theory made this a practical description of dendritic integration, especially when branching dendrites could be reduced to equivalent electrical trees under stated assumptions [@rall1962theory].

Synapses add chemical gating. Presynaptic depolarisation opens calcium channels; calcium promotes vesicle fusion; neurotransmitter crosses the cleft; receptors and transporters shape the postsynaptic current [@openstax2024neuroscience]. Electrical synapses, gap junctions, neuromodulators, glial uptake, and extracellular ion changes complicate the simple one-way picture. Cells also pattern themselves through slower fields. Reaction--diffusion systems, introduced by Turing, show how local activation and longer-range inhibition can produce spots, stripes, and developmental gradients without a pre-drawn template [@turing1952morphogenesis]. Mechanotransduction couples the cytoskeleton and extracellular matrix to gene regulation and cell shape [@ingber1997]. These processes are observed with voltage dyes, patch clamps, fluorescence calcium indicators, electron microscopy, molecular perturbation experiments, and optical reporters in living preparations and cultures. Microtubules, mitochondrial rhythms, calcium waves, and biophoton emissions are measurable biological phenomena, but their role as quantum-coherent carriers of cognition remains contested. Tegmark argued that warm wet neural tissue should decohere very rapidly [@tegmark2000importance], while Hameroff and Penrose propose the Orch OR microtubule hypothesis as a minority candidate rather than an established mechanism [@hameroff2014consciousness].

## How it was found and measured

Nineteenth-century electrophysiology established that nerves and muscles are electrical tissues; Bernstein's membrane theory gave an early ionic account of resting and active potentials [@bernstein1902untersuchungen]. In the twentieth century, intracellular electrodes, voltage clamp, and the large squid axon made the action potential mathematically measurable. Hodgkin and Huxley fitted conductance variables to voltage-clamp data in 1952, giving a reproducible dynamical model rather than a verbal metaphor [@hodgkin1952quantitative]. Katz's work on quantal transmitter release then tied synaptic events to discrete vesicle-scale mechanisms [@katz1969release]. Rall's dendritic cable theory connected anatomy to electrical integration across branching trees [@rall1962theory]. Later patch-clamp recording resolved single-channel currents directly, changing ion channels from inferred conductances into experimentally visible stochastic gates [@neher1976singlechannel]. Modern work combines electrophysiology with fluorescence microscopy, genetically encoded calcium and voltage indicators, optogenetics, connectomics, and molecular genetics. Each instrument trades one kind of access for another: electrodes measure current precisely but sparsely; optical methods see many cells but with slower or indirect signals; electron microscopy resolves synapses but usually in fixed tissue.

## Objects at this level

### Excitable membrane

An excitable membrane is a lipid boundary containing pumps, leak channels, voltage-gated channels, and capacitance. Its ordinary behaviour is governed by electrochemical gradients, channel kinetics, membrane resistance, and axial current flow. The [T]-Theory reading treats it as a local impulse-response boundary: a source current enters, and voltage returns as a filtered, thresholded response `interpretive`. It is not read as a feeling membrane or as standalone awareness `open-hypothesis`.

### Chemical synapse

A chemical synapse is a specialised junction where presynaptic activity changes postsynaptic current through vesicle release, transmitter diffusion, receptor binding, and clearance. Its strength depends on release probability, receptor number, history-dependent plasticity, and neuromodulation. The programme uses synaptic weight as an `interpretive` response parameter carried upward into network language; the molecular path from calcium sensor to receptor conformation is mostly integrated out `derived-under-assumptions`.

### Dendritic tree

A dendritic tree is a branching electrical and biochemical structure that receives thousands of synaptic inputs. Passive cable properties, active conductances, spine geometry, and local calcium dynamics decide whether an input remains local, summates, or helps trigger a spike. In [T]-Theory terms, dendrites are read as local Green-response geometries `interpretive`: shape and boundary conditions filter a source before the cell contributes to circuit-scale timing.

### Glial and extracellular interface

Astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, microglia, extracellular matrix, and ion-rich fluid are not passive background. They regulate transmitter uptake, potassium buffering, metabolic support, myelination, immune signalling, and extracellular volume. The [T]-Theory reading treats this interface as part of the cellular substrate that tunes coupling and damping `interpretive`. It does not claim that glia secretly replace neurons as the sole site of cognition `open-hypothesis`.

## The [T]-Theory reading

This level is read as a local bioelectric response grammar, not as a claim that single cells have subjective states `interpretive`. The substrate is membrane, channel, synapse, cytoskeleton, glial boundary, and nearby chemistry; the field variable is the voltage, calcium, or biochemical response to a perturbing source `interpretive`. The programme treats the cable Green's-function response as an `interpretive` small-scale instance of the impulse-in, response-out pattern developed in *The Soma-Field: A Wave-Based Model of Emotional Dynamics*. A pulse can be represented by its source, response kernel, decay, delay, and threshold crossing; those are the features that remain visible to the next scale `derived-under-assumptions`.

Synaptic weights and repeated cellular states can be discussed with Hopfield-memory language only as neural information storage, an `interpretive` mapping supported by ordinary network theory [@hopfield1982]. The cellular level is therefore a preparation stage for local-circuit dynamics: it supplies excitability, refractory periods, coupling strength, plasticity, and noise statistics `interpretive`. Molecular orbital detail, individual vesicle trajectories, and most thermal fluctuations are integrated out because many different microscopic histories can yield the same postsynaptic current `derived-under-assumptions`. Penrose--Hameroff microtubule language is retained only as an `open-hypothesis`, explicitly competing with fast-decoherence objections. Nothing in this entry says that a cultured cell has a person-like mind, that a synapse feels its own transmission, or that quantum coherence is required for ordinary spiking `open-hypothesis`.

## Open questions and tests

Ordinary science still debates how dendritic computation, glial regulation, extracellular fields, metabolic state, and neuromodulators jointly shape behaviour in intact tissue. Some mechanisms are well measured in slices or cultures but harder to isolate in a living brain. The strongest [T]-Theory test at this level would not be whether the cable equation exists; it does. The test would be whether the chosen response-kernel variables predict the next scale better than simpler voltage, rate, or conductance summaries `open-hypothesis`. A refutation route is equally clear: if cellular perturbations with matched standard electrophysiological variables but different proposed response-grammar variables produce no distinguishable circuit effects, the additional grammar is unnecessary `open-hypothesis`. Microtubule quantum claims require a separate test: sustained, functionally relevant coherence in warm neural tissue would need direct evidence against decoherence objections `open-hypothesis`.

Another practical test concerns compression. If a model can replace thousands of channel and synapse parameters with a smaller response kernel without losing predictive power, the scale transition is justified `derived-under-assumptions`. If the compression fails whenever conditions change, the registry should keep the richer cellular description rather than promote the simplified field reading `open-hypothesis`.
