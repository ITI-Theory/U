## In ordinary science

The observable universe is the region from which light or other signals have had time to reach us since the hot early universe. Its radius is about 46 billion light-years in comoving distance, larger than the age of the universe times the speed of light because space has expanded while the light travelled. The contents are described statistically: radiation, baryons, cold dark matter, dark energy, neutrinos, galaxies, clusters, voids, and the metric expansion.

The registry equation $\Box h_{\mu\nu}=-16\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}$ is the linearised Einstein equation. Stress-energy $T_{\mu\nu}$ sources a small metric perturbation $h_{\mu\nu}$, and $\Box$ is the spacetime wave operator. Full cosmology uses the Friedmann equations plus perturbation theory, but this equation captures the atlas idea that matter-energy and geometry respond to one another.

Planck 2018 reports a $\Lambda$CDM parameter set including $H_0=67.36$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_\Lambda=0.6847$, and cold dark matter $\Omega_c=0.2645$ [@planck2018cosmology]. Those numbers come from fitting CMB anisotropies and other data under a model. They sit beside, not outside, independent tensions and tests involving BAO, supernovae, lensing, cluster counts, and local distance ladders.

The observable universe is measured through the cosmic microwave background, galaxy surveys, gravitational lensing, primordial abundances, supernova distances, and large-scale simulations. Its key variables are scale factor, redshift, density parameters, power spectrum, horizon, curvature, and growth rate. It is an observational domain bounded by causal access, not the whole universe in an absolute metaphysical sense.

This boundary matters for language. Maps of the observable universe put the observer at the centre because all past-light-cone maps do, not because Earth is physically central. Distant shells show earlier epochs. A galaxy at high redshift is both far away and seen when the universe was younger, so the picture is a history as well as a spatial survey.
