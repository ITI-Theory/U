## In ordinary science

The cosmic web is the largest mapped pattern of matter: clusters at nodes, filaments as bridges, sheets as walls, and underdense voids between them. It is the nonlinear descendant of early-universe perturbations seen statistically in the cosmic microwave background and later in galaxy surveys. Gravity turns initially small density contrasts into a web whose topology depends on dark matter, baryons, expansion history, initial conditions, and the bias of the tracers used to map it [@peebles1980large; @springel2005millennium].

The registry equation $G_{20}(x,x')=\theta(t-t')\delta((x-x')^2)/(2\pi)$ is a retarded light-cone propagator. The step function $\theta(t-t')$ enforces that the response comes after the source; the delta term localises propagation on the light cone in the simplified expression. In real cosmology, propagation is shaped by expansion, curvature, gravitational potentials, scattering in the early plasma, and observation along the past light cone. The equation is therefore a compact atlas symbol for causality, not a complete structure-formation code.

The cosmic web spans hundreds to thousands of megaparsecs. Measurements include redshift surveys, baryon acoustic oscillation scales, weak lensing, cluster counts, CMB anisotropy, supernova distances, and simulations. Baryon acoustic oscillation features provide a standard ruler left by early acoustic waves in the photon-baryon plasma [@eisenstein2005bao]. Planck 2018 gives a flat $\Lambda$CDM baseline with $H_0=67.36$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_\Lambda=0.6847$, and $\Omega_c=0.2645$ [@planck2018cosmology].

This level needs horizon, selection-function, tracer-bias, and survey-volume variables. It is not merely a larger galaxy map; it is a causal and statistical reconstruction from finite, light-travel-time-limited data. A survey has magnitude limits, masks, redshift errors, fibre collisions, photometric uncertainties, and choices about which objects count as tracers. Simulations connect those imperfect catalogues to gravitational growth, while the CMB fixes early conditions against which late-time structure is compared.

The web is therefore both a map and a statistical object with uncertainties attached. It may be visually striking, but it is validated through correlation functions, power spectra, topology measures, void statistics, lensing maps, and repeated agreement between data and simulations. Its beauty is not the evidence; reproducible clustering is.

The web also links early and late cosmology. The same initial fluctuation spectrum that helps explain the CMB anisotropies is evolved forward into galaxies, haloes, and voids. Late-time dark energy slows structure growth relative to a matter-only universe, while dark matter supplies the gravitational scaffold on which baryons collect. For this reason, web measurements are never only cartography. They are tests of expansion, gravity, and the matter inventory.

## How it was found and measured

The cosmic web was anticipated by gravitational-instability theory and then seen in redshift surveys. The CfA "slice of the universe" in the 1980s showed galaxies arranged in walls and voids rather than evenly scattered points [@delapparent1986slice]. Bond, Kofman, and Pogosyan gave a clear theoretical account of filaments woven by the tidal field [@bond1996cosmicweb]. Large simulations, including the Millennium Simulation, then made web formation from cold dark matter initial conditions visually and statistically explicit [@springel2005millennium].

Modern measurements combine galaxy redshifts, weak lensing, cluster catalogues, BAO standard rulers, and CMB constraints. The BAO peak detected in Sloan Digital Sky Survey luminous red galaxies gave a robust standard ruler in the late-time galaxy distribution [@eisenstein2005bao]. Planck fixed the early-universe parameters against which the web's later growth is compared [@planck2018cosmology].

## Objects at this level

### Web node

A web node is a dense junction, usually hosting a cluster or group where several filaments meet. It is governed by gravitational collapse, merger history, and tidal flow. Nodes are important for cluster growth and environmental effects on galaxies. In [T]-Theory terms, a node is a convergence of response channels under coarse-graining, not a command point or conscious centre `interpretive`.

### Wall or sheet

A wall or sheet is a broad, flattened overdensity between voids. It is governed by anisotropic collapse: matter collapses first along one direction, leaving a sheet before later filament and node formation. Its boundaries depend on density thresholds and survey tracers. The [T]-Theory reading treats a wall as a lower-dimensional response surface in the matter field `interpretive`.

### Cosmic void

A void is a large underdense region surrounded by sheets, filaments, and nodes. Voids are governed by gravitational evacuation, expansion, and the growth of surrounding structure. They test dark energy, modified gravity, and galaxy bias because emptiness has dynamics too. The [T]-Theory overlay treats voids as boundary conditions in the response graph, not as empty metaphysical spaces `interpretive`.

### BAO standard ruler

The baryon acoustic oscillation scale is a statistical feature, not a single object. It is the frozen remnant of sound waves in the early photon-baryon plasma, later seen as a preferred separation in galaxy clustering. It is governed by early-universe plasma physics and later cosmic expansion. [T]-Theory may use it as a clean response example only when the standard physics remains primary `interpretive`.

## Open questions and tests

Ordinary web science is limited by survey selection, tracer bias, nonlinear baryonic effects, and tensions among cosmological probes. The $H_0$ tension, possible differences between weak-lensing and CMB-inferred clustering amplitudes, and the role of feedback in reshaping matter power remain active topics. Future surveys test the web with larger volumes, better redshifts, deeper lensing, and cross-correlations among tracers.

The [T]-Theory reading could be tested by whether its response graph predicts robust coarse-grained quantities across survey choices `open-hypothesis`. It should say which topological or propagator variables remain invariant when the tracer changes, and whether the $7/11$ or $3/11$ comparisons improve any fit without being tuned after the fact `open-hypothesis`. Otherwise it remains an interpretive display layer `interpretive`.

The atlas should therefore resist a common over-reading `interpretive`: a connected map is not automatically an organism, and a causal propagator is not automatically experience. A failed prediction, a loss of invariant topology, or a better ordinary model with no programme term would count against stronger claims `open-hypothesis`.
