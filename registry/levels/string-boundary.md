## In ordinary science

The string-boundary level represents a theoretical band just above the Planck boundary, where some quantum-gravity models replace point particles with one-dimensional extended objects. In perturbative string theory a string sweeps out a two-dimensional worldsheet, and different vibrational modes are interpreted as different particle states. Closed strings include a spin-2 mode with the right properties for a graviton; open strings can end on D-branes, where gauge fields arise in many models [@polchinski1998string1; @witten1995].

The ordinary equation shown in the registry is the free worldsheet Green's function,
$$G_{\mathrm{string}}(s,s')=-\frac{\alpha'}{2}\log |s-s'|^2.$$
In words, $\alpha'$ is related to string tension, $s$ and $s'$ are worldsheet coordinates, and the logarithm describes the response of a two-dimensional field to a local source on the worldsheet. This is not an image of a tiny violin string. It is a quantum field theory on a surface, with boundary conditions determining which modes are allowed.

M-theory is the name for a conjectural unifying framework in which the five ten-dimensional superstring theories appear as different limits of an eleven-dimensional theory [@witten1995]. Horava-Witten theory is one important boundary construction: eleven-dimensional supergravity is studied on a manifold with boundary, leading to gauge degrees of freedom on those boundaries [@horava1996]. These are mathematical and high-energy theoretical structures. They are not direct observations at $10^{-32}$ m, and they do not by themselves select the observed low-energy universe.

Typical string lengths depend on the model and need not equal the Planck length exactly; the registry scale should therefore be read as an order-of-magnitude theoretical boundary, not a measured ruler mark. The measured world at accessible energies is still described by quantum field theory and the Standard Model. The string-boundary level is included because it supplies a well-studied example of propagators, modes, compactification, branes, and boundary conditions before ordinary particles are treated as localised excitations.
