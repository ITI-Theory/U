## In ordinary science

The quantum-foam level names the Planck boundary, near $\ell_P \approx 1.6\times10^{-35}$ m and $t_P \approx 5.4\times10^{-44}$ s. At that scale the separate frameworks that work at larger distances--quantum field theory on a fixed background and general relativity as a smooth geometry--are both being pushed beyond their tested domain. "Foam" is therefore a cautious term: it is a heuristic for metric and topology fluctuations, not a photographed substance or a directly measured medium [@wheeler1957geometrodynamics].

The measured physics below this boundary is indirect. Particle experiments test quantum fields and scattering amplitudes at energies far below the Planck energy; cosmology tests early-universe models through radiation, expansion, and structure formation. The Planck scale itself is inferred from $G$, $\hbar$, and $c$, where gravity, quantum action, and relativity become comparable. The resulting energy, $E_P\approx1.22\times10^{19}$ GeV, is far beyond current accelerators, so ordinary statements about this level must distinguish calculation from observation.

A useful ordinary equation is the propagator amplitude
$$G_P(x,x')=\langle x\mid G\mid x'\rangle,$$
which says, in words, that the theory asks for an amplitude connecting a source configuration $x'$ to an observed configuration $x$. In Feynman's path-integral form, amplitudes are built by summing alternatives with phases $e^{iS/\hbar}$, where $S$ is the action along a history [@feynman1948; @glimm1987quantum].

Unlike the molecular or atomic levels, there is no stable object here with a laboratory image. The level is defined by the failure of ordinary smoothness: distance, duration, and curvature cannot be treated as classical background quantities all the way down. Different quantum-gravity programmes give different candidate substrates--spin networks, causal sets, strings, loops, or path integrals over geometries--and none is yet empirically settled. The atlas therefore treats this level as a boundary of present theory. What survives as ordinary science is the operator grammar: a local source, a propagator, boundary conditions, and a probability amplitude. What does not survive is any claim that the vacuum is literally a mental field, a visible froth, or an experimentally resolved lattice.

The phrase "quantum foam" is therefore a signpost rather than a settled ontology. It marks the scale at which known equations say their own background assumptions have become unsafe. In ordinary science the safest language is still mathematical: amplitudes, histories, constraints, and the need for a quantum theory of geometry.

This caution matters for scale. Below molecules or atoms, a detector can still register tracks, photons, charges, or spectra. At the Planck boundary the detector itself would be a gravitational participant in the event being probed. That is why ordinary descriptions lean on invariants, dimensional scales, and consistency conditions instead of direct pictures. The atlas keeps this level sparse because too much visual detail would falsely imply observation.

## How it was found and measured

The scale begins with Max Planck's 1900 quantum of action, later combined with $G$ and $c$ to form natural units [@planck1900quantum]. Einstein's general relativity then made spacetime geometry dynamical, and quantum theory made amplitudes rather than definite paths central to microscopic prediction. Wheeler introduced the phrase "quantum foam" in the mid twentieth century as a way to describe how violent metric fluctuations might appear near the Planck length [@wheeler1957geometrodynamics].

No instrument has imaged Planck-scale foam. Instead, the boundary is inferred by dimensional analysis and by the incompatibility of applying smooth classical geometry indefinitely. Laboratory tests constrain Lorentz symmetry, short-distance gravity, and high-energy scattering; astronomical observations constrain early-universe and high-energy models. None gives a direct picture of topology change or discrete geometry. The measured fact is the success of quantum field theory and general relativity in their domains; the foam is a name for the expected breakdown zone where a deeper theory is required.

Modern proposals look for indirect traces: modified dispersion of high-energy photons, primordial gravitational-wave backgrounds, black-hole evaporation signatures, or departures from inverse-square gravity at small distances. So far these are constraints, not detections. The historical lesson is negative but important: the Planck scale is not reached by making a better microscope alone, because concentration of enough energy would itself curve spacetime strongly.

## Objects at this level

### Planck boundary

The Planck boundary is not an object in the usual sense. It is the order of length, time, and energy where $G$, $\hbar$, and $c$ become equally important. Ordinary science treats it as a limit of extrapolation from tested theories. The [T]-Theory reading uses it as a lower bookkeeping boundary for response grammar only `interpretive`: an impulse and kernel can be written, but no physical mind, feeling, or organism is assigned.

### Candidate geometric histories

Path-integral approaches describe possible histories of fields or geometries weighted by action. At this level the "object" is a family of alternatives rather than a particle with a track. Boundary conditions and constraints decide which histories contribute to an amplitude. The [T]-Theory reading keeps only that selection grammar `derived-under-assumptions`: alternatives are compared by a kernel, while any claim that the alternatives are experiential remains excluded `interpretive`.

### Vacuum fluctuation language

Vacuum fluctuations in quantum field theory are measurable through effects such as radiative corrections and the Casimir force, but those are not direct measurements of Planck foam. They are lower-energy evidence that the vacuum is not classical emptiness. The atlas keeps the distinction. [T]-Theory may use "fluctuation" as a response metaphor `interpretive`, but the ordinary object here is a quantum state and its correlations, not a visible substance.

A valid use of this level must name the projection step and the evidence label for the new substrate.

## Open questions and tests

Ordinary open questions include the correct quantum theory of gravity, whether spacetime is emergent, whether locality changes near the Planck scale, and how singularities are avoided. Direct tests are difficult because the energy scale is enormous. Constraints may come from cosmology, black-hole thermodynamics, gravitational-wave signatures, high-precision Lorentz tests, or mathematical consistency.

For [T]-Theory, the discriminating test is conceptual and formal before it is empirical `open-hypothesis`. The programme must state which kernel is being used, what is integrated out, and what prediction changes if another quantum-gravity substrate is chosen. If all lower-boundary choices produce the same prose, the level is only a metaphor. If different kernels lead to distinct transition rules in the atlas or app, the claim becomes testable as a modelling architecture `open-hypothesis`.

An especially clear refutation would be internal: if the same renderer or text treated incompatible quantum-gravity assumptions as interchangeable without changing variables, the level would fail its own evidence discipline. A stronger future version would compare at least two candidate kernels and document how the next transition differs.

- **Lens off:** Show a dark Planck-scale boundary with no stable objects: small fluctuating patches of geometry, short-lived loops, and a scale bar at $10^{-35}$ m. Avoid gears, brains, faces, or particles with hard outlines.
