## In ordinary science

The quantum-foam level names the Planck boundary, near $\ell_P \approx 1.6\times10^{-35}$ m and $t_P \approx 5.4\times10^{-44}$ s. At that scale the separate frameworks that work at larger distances--quantum field theory on a fixed background and general relativity as a smooth geometry--are both being pushed beyond their tested domain. "Foam" is therefore a cautious term: it is a heuristic for metric and topology fluctuations, not a photographed substance or a directly measured medium [@wheeler1957geometrodynamics].

The measurable physics below this boundary is indirect. Particle experiments test quantum fields and scattering amplitudes at energies far below the Planck energy; cosmology tests early-universe models through radiation, expansion, and structure formation. The Planck scale itself is inferred from $G$, $\hbar$, and $c$, where gravity, quantum action, and relativity become comparable. A useful ordinary equation is the propagator amplitude
$$G_P(x,x')=\langle x\mid G\mid x'\rangle,$$
which says, in words, that the theory asks for an amplitude connecting a source configuration $x'$ to an observed configuration $x$. In Feynman's path-integral form, amplitudes are built by summing alternatives with phases $e^{iS/\hbar}$, where $S$ is the action along a history [@feynman1948; @glimm1987quantum].

Unlike the molecular or atomic levels, there is no stable object here with a laboratory image. The level is defined by the failure of ordinary smoothness: distance, duration, and curvature cannot be treated as classical background quantities all the way down. Different quantum-gravity programmes give different candidate substrates--spin networks, causal sets, strings, loops, or path integrals over geometries--and none is yet empirically settled. The atlas therefore treats this level as a boundary of present theory. What survives as ordinary science is the operator grammar: a local source, a propagator, boundary conditions, and a probability amplitude. What does not survive is any claim that the vacuum is literally a mental field, a visible froth, or an experimentally resolved lattice.

Typical energies are Planckian, $E_P\approx1.22\times10^{19}$ GeV, enormously above accelerator scales. Typical times are Planck times, so ordinary causal stories have to be stated with care. Quantum foam is best read as the scale at which known equations say their own background assumptions have become unsafe.

## The [T]-Theory reading

In *The Zoomable Universal Somatic Field: A Scale-Invariant Green's Function Architecture*, this level is used as the lower boundary of the shared response grammar `interpretive` [@johnsonzsf2026]. The substrate is not assigned feelings or agency; it is a bookkeeping limit for possible configurations `interpretive`. The field is read as a Green-kernel amplitude: an impulse in a pre-geometric state is paired with a propagator response out `derived-under-assumptions`.

The programme carries upward the source-response pattern--source, kernel, boundary condition, coarse-grained response--while integrating out any claim that Planck-scale alternatives are visible classical objects `interpretive`. The formal habit of writing $G_P(x,x')$ is a model choice, not a measurement of foam `derived-under-assumptions`. No consciousness, affect, or emotion is claimed at this scale; the atlas claims only that the same notational grammar can be reused when later levels acquire empirical substrates `open-hypothesis`.

## The pictures

- **Lens off:** Show a dark Planck-scale boundary with no stable objects: small fluctuating patches of geometry, short-lived loops, and a scale bar at $10^{-35}$ m. Avoid gears, brains, faces, or particles with hard outlines.
- **Lens on:** Add a faint source point and many translucent possible response paths, converging into a single labelled kernel $G_P(x,x')$. The overlay should look like probability amplitude bookkeeping, not like a classical wave in a material fluid.
