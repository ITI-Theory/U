## In ordinary science

A human vertebrate is a single living organism whose behaviour is produced by coupled body, brain, endocrine, immune, and environmental loops. The ordinary substrate includes musculoskeletal tensegrity, viscera, cardiovascular rhythms, peripheral nerves, spinal cord, brainstem, limbic circuits, cortex, and the weak electromagnetic fields generated by neural and cardiac currents. None of these layers is sufficient on its own. Interoception reports the physiological condition of the body to the nervous system; emotion and action selection depend on this traffic as well as on cortical appraisal, memory, movement, and social context [@craig2003; @damasio1994; @openstax2024neuroscience].

At this scale the useful variables are slow compared with molecular chemistry but fast compared with biography. Neural spikes and local field potentials change over milliseconds; cardiac intervals are around one second; respiratory cycles usually take several seconds; posture, gaze, muscle tone, and endocrine pulses can change over seconds to minutes. ECG and EEG/MEG measure electrical activity at the surface or outside the body. HRV summarises variation in beat-to-beat timing and is shaped by respiration, baroreflexes, metabolic demand, and autonomic regulation [@grossman2007toward; @bauer2006]. CEMI proposals ask whether endogenous electromagnetic fields are causally relevant to neural integration, but mainstream neuroscience treats them cautiously and separates measurement of fields from claims about consciousness [@mcfadden2002a; @mcfadden2002b].

The registry equation, $\gamma\dot e=-\nabla H(e)+\sqrt{2D}\,\xi(t)+J(t)$, is a standard noisy-gradient or Langevin form. In words, the state $e$ relaxes down a landscape $H$ with damping $\gamma$; random fluctuations enter through $\xi(t)$ with strength $D$; and an external or internal input enters as $J(t)$. Hopfield networks give the ordinary mathematical precedent for attractor landscapes in neural computation: stored patterns appear as minima of an energy function, and trajectories tend to settle into nearby basins [@hopfield1982; @hertz1991]. This is a modelling analogy, not a claim that the whole organism literally stores memories as one neural network. It is useful because many organismal responses are neither free choices nor fixed reflexes: they are constrained trajectories through a body with inertia, noise, feedback, and history.

The energy budget is metabolic, electrical, mechanical, and thermal at once. Food and oxygen support ion gradients and muscle work; cardiac and respiratory systems transport gases and heat; fascia, tendon, and bone transmit loads; sensory organs sample a changing environment. Music and rhythm can act as time-varying sensory inputs, but affective response to music is variable, culturally shaped, task-dependent, and not a clinical intervention by itself [@juslin2010; @juslin2013]. The ordinary organism is therefore a multi-loop control system whose measurable outputs include motion, breath, blood flow, electrical traces, hormone levels, skin conductance, voice, and reported experience.

## How it was found and measured

The human/vertebrate level was not discovered in one event. It was assembled from anatomy, physiology, electrophysiology, ethology, psychology, and control theory. Nineteenth-century physiology established that nerves, muscles, and hearts have measurable electrical activity. Willem Einthoven's string galvanometer made the electrocardiogram a reproducible body-surface measurement in the early twentieth century [@einthoven1903galvanometrische]. Hans Berger reported the human electroencephalogram in 1929, opening a cautious route from scalp voltages to brain rhythms [@berger1929ueber]. Walter Cannon's homeostasis work then made bodily regulation a central physiological concept rather than a background condition [@cannon1929organization].

Later instrumentation widened the organismal record. Strain gauges, spirometry, blood-pressure cuffs, impedance methods, accelerometers, motion capture, EEG, MEG, ECG, electromyography, fMRI, endocrine assays, and wearable sensors all sample different parts of the same living system. None gives a full state vector. Interoception research clarified that signals from viscera and body tissues shape feeling, attention, and action [@craig2003]. HRV research showed that a simple sequence of beat intervals contains respiratory, baroreflex, and autonomic structure, while also warning that interpretation depends strongly on window length and context [@grossman2007toward]. The level is therefore empirical, but its integrated state variables are constructed from partial observations.

## Objects at this level

### Vertebrate organism

The central object is the bounded organism: a body with skin, skeleton, viscera, nervous system, immune function, endocrine regulation, and behaviour in an environment. Classical science governs it through anatomy, physiology, biomechanics, metabolism, development, and ecology. The [T]-Theory reading treats the organism as the scale where a response landscape may be drawn for affective dynamics `interpretive`; the boundary remains the biological body, not an aura or diagnostic category `open-hypothesis`.

### Cardiorespiratory rhythm

Cardiorespiratory rhythm includes heart timing, breathing, blood pressure, gas exchange, and their reflex coupling. Classical analysis uses ECG, respiration belts, blood-pressure traces, and spectral or time-domain summaries such as HRV. The [T]-Theory reading uses these rhythms as observable forcing and damping channels in the organismal state equation `interpretive`. It does not infer health, diagnosis, or treatment effect from a trace alone `open-hypothesis`.

### Interoceptive loop

An interoceptive loop carries information about the body's internal condition through peripheral receptors, spinal and brainstem pathways, thalamic and cortical processing, and behavioural response. Classical accounts connect it to homeostasis, prediction, and action selection. The programme reads it as one route by which $J(t)$ and the landscape $H$ can change `derived-under-assumptions`. The mapping is testable only when the measured signals and task are specified `open-hypothesis`.

### Biosignal record

A biosignal record is not the organism itself. It is a sampled electrical, mechanical, chemical, or behavioural trace: EEG, ECG, respiration, motion capture, voice, skin conductance, hormone assay, or similar data. Classical interpretation depends on sensors, filters, sampling rate, and artefact rejection. The [T]-Theory reading treats such records as projections of a richer state `interpretive`; it should never promote a projection into a complete mind-state `open-hypothesis`.

## The [T]-Theory reading

The programme treats the human/vertebrate level as its centre: affect is modelled as an organism-scale field, not as a single brain variable `interpretive`. The state $e$ is read as an eight-component somatic coordinate, the landscape $H$ as a Hopfield-style attractor landscape, and $J(t)$ as touch, sound, breath, posture, memory, movement, or social forcing `derived-under-assumptions`. *The Missing Limbic Layer: A Somatic Field Extension of Hopfield Networks via the Correspondence Principle* and *The Soma-Field: A Wave-Based Model of Emotional Dynamics* use this level to connect body substrate, limbic response, and attractor geometry `interpretive`. *The Physical Substrate of the Soma-Field* motivates body and interoceptive carriers, but the substrate assignment remains a model choice `open-hypothesis`.

The response grammar is impulse in, damped trajectory out `interpretive`. A sound, breath change, posture shift, remembered cue, or interaction can be represented as forcing if it is operationalised. The organism then answers through measurable channels such as timing, motion, autonomic state, attention, and report `derived-under-assumptions`. What is carried upward from lower levels is neural and bodily response; what is integrated out is the molecular and tissue detail that does not appear in the chosen state vector `interpretive`. The model is intentionally bounded by the organism. It does not replace neuroscience, physiology, psychology, or ethology, and it does not claim that every body has the same attractor landscape `open-hypothesis`.

QUANT-EXP-1 is not evidence for therapy, hardware speed-up, or consciousness. It is an exact 8-qubit statevector simulation of reachability in a toy attractor landscape `simulated`. The level does not claim a running clinical study, a diagnosis, a treatment outcome, or proof that consciousness is a threshold crossing `open-hypothesis`. The safe claim is narrower: the programme defines a formal response vocabulary for organism-scale affective dynamics and asks whether that vocabulary can be connected to data without over-reading the data `open-hypothesis`.

## Open questions and tests

The ordinary open questions are already substantial. Which interoceptive, neural, musculoskeletal, and environmental variables are necessary for a useful organismal state? How should reports, behaviour, and physiology be combined without circularity? How much of affective dynamics is person-specific, species-specific, task-specific, or culturally learned? The [T]-Theory reading adds sharper tests `open-hypothesis`. An eight-coordinate model should predict held-out biosignal or behavioural trajectories better than lower-dimensional or purely descriptive baselines `open-hypothesis`. A proposed forcing term $J(t)$ should have a declared sensor, timing, and perturbation protocol `derived-under-assumptions`. The landscape $H$ should make falsifiable predictions about recovery time, hysteresis, or basin switching rather than only naming states after the fact `open-hypothesis`.
