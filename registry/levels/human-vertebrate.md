## In ordinary science

A human vertebrate is a single living organism whose behaviour is produced by coupled body, brain, endocrine, immune, and environmental loops. The ordinary substrate includes musculoskeletal tensegrity, viscera, cardiovascular rhythms, peripheral nerves, spinal cord, brainstem, limbic circuits, cortex, and the weak electromagnetic fields generated by neural and cardiac currents. None of these layers is sufficient on its own. Interoception reports the physiological condition of the body to the nervous system; emotion and action selection depend on this traffic as well as on cortical appraisal and memory [@craig2003; @damasio1994; @openstax2024neuroscience].

At this scale the useful variables are slow compared with molecular chemistry but fast compared with biography: heart periods of about one second, breathing periods of a few seconds, postural adjustments over seconds to minutes, and neural events from milliseconds to seconds. ECG and EEG/MEG measure electrical activity at the surface or outside the body; HRV summarises variation in beat-to-beat timing and is shaped by respiration, baroreflexes, and autonomic regulation [@grossman2007toward; @bauer2006]. CEMI proposals ask whether endogenous electromagnetic fields are causally relevant to neural integration, but mainstream neuroscience treats them cautiously and separates measurement from claims about consciousness [@mcfadden2002a; @mcfadden2002b].

The registry equation, $\gamma\dot e=-\nabla H(e)+\sqrt{2D}\,\xi(t)+J(t)$, is a standard noisy-gradient or Langevin form. In words, the state $e$ relaxes down a landscape $H$ with damping $\gamma$; random fluctuations enter through $\xi(t)$ with strength $D$; and an external or internal input enters as $J(t)$. Hopfield networks give the ordinary mathematical precedent for attractor landscapes in neural computation: stored patterns appear as minima of an energy function, and trajectories tend to settle into nearby basins [@hopfield1982; @hertz1991]. Music and rhythm can also act as time-varying sensory inputs, but affective response to music is variable, culturally shaped, and not a clinical intervention by itself [@juslin2010; @juslin2013].

## The [T]-Theory reading

The programme treats the human/vertebrate level as its centre: affect is modelled as an organism-scale field, not as a single brain variable `interpretive`. The state $e$ is read as an eight-component somatic coordinate, the landscape $H$ as a Hopfield-style attractor landscape, and $J(t)$ as touch, sound, breath, posture, memory, or social forcing `derived-under-assumptions`. *The Missing Limbic Layer: A Somatic Field Extension of Hopfield Networks via the Correspondence Principle* and *The Soma-Field: A Wave-Based Model of Emotional Dynamics* use this level to connect body substrate, limbic response, and attractor geometry `interpretive`. *The Physical Substrate of the Soma-Field* motivates body and interoceptive carriers, but the substrate assignment remains a model choice `open-hypothesis`. QUANT-EXP-1 is not evidence for therapy or consciousness; it is only an exact 8-qubit statevector simulation of reachability in a toy attractor landscape `simulated`. The level does not claim a running clinical study, a diagnosis, or proof that consciousness is a threshold crossing `open-hypothesis`.
