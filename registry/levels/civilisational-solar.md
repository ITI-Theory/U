## In ordinary science

This registry level is deliberately hybrid. There is no single ordinary-science object called a civilisational-solar system. The YAML joins two earlier ladder branches that both involve large, slow, path-dependent systems. On the social branch, civilisations are studied through settlement systems, states, institutions, energy use, demography, trade, records, conflict, and technological lock-in. On the orbital branch, solar-system dynamics studies bodies moving under gravity around a star. The comparison is useful for an atlas of scale, but the two branches are not the same physical system.

The displayed equation, $\nabla^2\Phi=4\pi G\rho$, is Poisson's equation for Newtonian gravity. In words, the gravitational potential $\Phi$ is sourced by mass density $\rho$, with $G$ setting the strength of gravity. It belongs cleanly to the orbital branch: planets, moons, asteroids, and spacecraft move in a potential shaped by masses and boundary conditions [@murray1999solar]. Characteristic distances run from planetary radii to astronomical units, and characteristic times range from days to billions of years.

The civilisational branch uses different equations. It works with population dynamics, resource flows, institutional persistence, network models, economic feedbacks, and historical data. There is no accepted Poisson equation for civilisation. A cautious shared vocabulary is attractor landscape: some configurations become stable because roads, laws, stories, military systems, energy infrastructures, and archives make them easier to maintain than to leave. Institutional economics describes these persistent constraints without treating them as forces in the physical sense [@north1990institutions]. Historical-dynamics programmes attempt quantitative models of long social change, but their assumptions and data quality must be inspected carefully [@turchin2003historical].

The ordinary-science lesson is therefore twofold. Solar systems show real gravitational basins and resonances. Civilisations show durable social basins and path dependence. Both require attention to initial conditions and to slow variables, but the units, evidence, and equations differ. The Atlas keeps them at one broad scale for navigation, while keeping their mechanisms separate.

## The [T]-Theory reading

`interpretive`: The programme treats civilisational-solar as a reconciliation layer, not an identity claim. `derived-under-assumptions`: The orbital branch may use gravitational potential and long-lived basin structure; the civilisational branch may use attractor language for records, infrastructure, law, and energy regimes. `interpretive`: The hybrid exists because the app's ladder preserves both the social continuation from region and the physical continuation toward planets. `interpretive`: What is carried upward is slow constraint, not a common material substrate, and the two branches may diverge. `open-hypothesis`: Any cross-branch analogy must state which variables are being compared and what would count against it empirically. The level does not claim that societies orbit like planets, that solar systems have culture, or that civilisation is governed by Newtonian gravity.
