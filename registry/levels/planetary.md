## In ordinary science

The planetary level treats a whole planet as a coupled physical system: solid interior, mantle, crust, ocean, atmosphere, cryosphere, ionosphere, magnetosphere, and space environment. Measurable variables include gravity, topography, heat flow, mantle viscosity, plate velocity, pressure, wind, temperature, humidity, salinity, sea level, magnetic field, albedo, greenhouse-gas concentration, and radiative flux. Lengths run from hundreds to thousands of kilometres; times run from minutes for atmospheric waves, months to years for ocean climate modes, and millions of years for mantle and carbon-cycle feedbacks.

The registry equation, $\rho(D_t\mathbf u)=-\nabla P+\eta\nabla^2\mathbf u+\rho\mathbf g$, is a Navier--Stokes-style momentum balance with gravity. In words, fluid or slowly deforming material changes velocity because pressure gradients push it, viscosity diffuses momentum, and gravity drives buoyancy and weight. Rotating planets add Coriolis terms, stratification, phase changes, radiation, chemistry, and boundary conditions. The equation is therefore a doorway into a family of geophysical-fluid and mantle-flow models, not a complete climate model by itself [@vallis2017atmospheric; @holton2013introduction].

Planetary science includes familiar wave and feedback phenomena. Rossby waves organise large-scale weather and ocean circulation. ENSO couples tropical Pacific winds, sea-surface temperature, and thermocline depth. Internal waves move energy through stratified oceans. Mantle convection moves heat from the interior. The magnetosphere responds to solar wind. Climate models combine fluid dynamics, radiation, clouds, aerosols, biogeochemistry, land surface, ice, and human forcing [@ipcc2021ar6wg1]. Tipping-element research asks when feedbacks can shift parts of the Earth system into new regimes [@lenton2008tipping].

The ordinary scientific boundary is explicit: planets regulate energy and material flows, but they do not need consciousness to do so. Stability, oscillation, and transition are physical system properties. Planetary measurements are also multi-instrument: seismometers, satellites, ocean floats, weather stations, magnetometers, ice cores, and geochemical archives each sample a different layer of the coupled system over deep planetary time.
