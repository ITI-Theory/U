## In ordinary science

A galactic halo is the extended region around a galaxy containing dark matter, old stars, globular clusters, hot gas, satellite galaxies, and tidal debris. Much of the halo is inferred rather than directly imaged. Rotation curves stay flatter than the visible mass alone predicts; lensing, satellite motions, stellar streams, and hot-gas measurements all help reconstruct the mass distribution [@rubin1980rotation; @binney2008galactic].

The registry equation $\nabla^2\Phi_{16}=4\pi G\rho_{\mathrm{halo}}$ is Poisson's equation specialised to halo density. The source is the extended mass density $\rho_{\mathrm{halo}}$; the response is the gravitational potential $\Phi_{16}$ that controls orbits and light deflection. In the standard cosmological model, cold dark matter haloes grow hierarchically and are often approximated by profiles such as the Navarro-Frenk-White form, though baryonic feedback, self-interaction limits, and galaxy formation history complicate real systems [@navarro1997nfw].

Typical halo radii extend to hundreds of kiloparsecs for large galaxies. Timescales are long: orbital periods, merger histories, satellite infall, and stream wrapping last hundreds of millions to billions of years. The halo needs variables that a disc alone cannot supply: virial mass, concentration, anisotropy, subhalo population, circumgalactic gas, and lensing shear.

Dark matter is measured by its gravitational effects, not by direct laboratory detection in this context. Modified gravity remains a live comparison class for some phenomena, but cluster lensing, cosmology, and structure formation make the dark-sector question broader than any one rotation curve.

Halo science is therefore an inference problem with several cross-checks. A stellar stream can reveal lumps in the potential; weak lensing stacks many galaxies to estimate average mass; satellite counts test small-scale structure. The halo is not drawn because it shines like a shell. It is reconstructed because many independent tracers curve, shear, or disperse as if an extended mass distribution is present.

That is why the halo belongs with fields and distributions, not ordinary visible surfaces.

## The [T]-Theory reading

The programme reads the halo as an unseen but measurable response layer: visible orbits and lensing are the returned signal of an extended source distribution `interpretive` [@johnson2026usf]. It carries up disc rotation, satellites, streams, and merger history, while individual stellar and gas-cloud details are integrated out into a halo potential `derived-under-assumptions`.

*Dark Matter as the Spatial Vacuum of the Universal Somatic Field: Ω_DM = 3/11* compares a model value $3/11$ with Planck 2018's cold-dark-matter value $\Omega_c=0.2645$; this is model-dependent, not an empirical derivation `derived-under-assumptions` [@johnson2026darkmatter; @planck2018cosmology]. The entry does not claim a hidden subject or feeling in the halo. It claims only response bookkeeping `interpretive`.

The distinction between inferred mass and interpreted field language should remain visible in the app and in print `interpretive`.

## The pictures

- **Lens off:** Show a bright galactic disc inside a much larger transparent halo. Add satellite galaxies, stellar streams, and a rotation-curve inset comparing visible-mass and observed speeds across the same radius and beyond the optical edge, clearly marked outward.
- **Lens on:** Overlay a smooth Poisson potential and faint subhalo knots. Let orbital tracks and lensing arcs brighten where the unseen mass distribution changes the visible response, while keeping the halo non-luminous, inferred, labelled, and bounded by a faint virial-radius ring.
