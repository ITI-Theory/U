## In ordinary science

A galaxy cluster is a gravitationally bound structure containing hundreds to thousands of galaxies, hot intracluster plasma, dark matter, and diffuse light. Most ordinary baryonic mass in rich clusters is not in the galaxies but in X-ray emitting gas at tens of millions of kelvin. Most total gravitating mass is dark matter, inferred through galaxy velocities, X-ray hydrostatic estimates, Sunyaev-Zel'dovich measurements, and gravitational lensing [@sarazin1986xray; @clowe2006bullet].

The registry equation $(\partial_\eta^2+2\mathcal H\partial_\eta-c_s^2\nabla^2)G_{17}=\delta^{(4)}$ describes a cosmological perturbation response in conformal time $\eta$. The $2\mathcal H\partial_\eta$ term represents damping by expansion, $c_s$ is an effective sound speed, and the delta function marks an impulse. For actual cluster astrophysics, this large-scale equation sits beside gas dynamics, collisionless dynamics, plasma physics, and lensing geometry.

Cluster sizes are megaparsecs, and dynamical times are billions of years. Mergers drive shocks, cold fronts, radio relics, turbulence, and offsets among galaxies, gas, and lensing mass. The Bullet Cluster is famous because the X-ray gas and lensing mass are spatially separated, strengthening the case that much cluster mass behaves differently from collisional gas [@clowe2006bullet].

A cluster therefore needs variables that a single galaxy does not: mass function, richness, temperature profile, gas fraction, lensing convergence, merger state, and location in the larger cosmic web.

Clusters are also precision cosmology tools. Their abundance and growth depend on the amplitude of matter fluctuations and the expansion history, while their gas fraction and lensing masses test structure formation. The difficulty is calibration: selection effects, non-thermal pressure, merger disturbance, and projection can bias a mass estimate. Cluster science therefore compares multiple observables rather than trusting a single image.

This makes the cluster a laboratory for separating collisional gas from nearly collisionless galaxies and dark matter.

Those separations are visible only because the components respond differently.

## The [T]-Theory reading

The programme reads a cluster as a multi-component response field in which galaxies, hot gas, and dark matter return different signals to the same gravitational history `interpretive` [@johnson2026usf]. It carries up halo potentials, disc populations, and merger histories, while individual galactic morphologies are integrated out into mass, gas, lensing, and richness variables `derived-under-assumptions`.

The cosmology-facing papers may compare large-scale fractions with the programme's tensor bookkeeping `derived-under-assumptions` [@johnson2026cosconst; @johnson2026darkmatter]. They do not replace cluster observations. What is not claimed: a cluster has no awareness in this entry, and offsets between gas and lensing are not treated as evidence for a somatic mind. They are gravitational and plasma data first `empirical-result`.

The [T]-Theory layer is a reading of response separation across components, not a new detection of dark matter `interpretive`.
