## In ordinary science

The whole-brain scale covers coordinated neural activity across cortical layers, subcortical nuclei, white-matter tracts, cerebrospinal fluid, vasculature, and the skull boundary. The characteristic length is decimetres; the timescales range from millisecond spikes to seconds-long rhythms and minutes-long state changes. EEG and MEG measure population activity indirectly: synchronised postsynaptic currents produce electric potentials and magnetic fields that can be detected outside the head, although inverse reconstruction is ill-posed and spatially filtered [@nolte2004identifying].

The registry equation $(\nabla^2+k_{\mathrm{CEMI}}^2)G=\delta$ is a Helmholtz-style Green-function display. In words, the spatial curvature of a response $G$ plus a wavelength-setting term $k_{\mathrm{CEMI}}^2$ balances a point source $\delta$. This is a compact way to picture how a local neural source can contribute to a macroscopic electromagnetic pattern. It is not, by itself, proof that electromagnetic fields are the seat of consciousness.

Mainstream neuroscience already uses field language carefully. Local field potentials reflect summed transmembrane currents; coherence and phase coupling can mark communication between areas; and rhythms may regulate when signals are likely to affect downstream targets [@fries2005mechanism]. CEMI theory, associated especially with McFadden, is a stronger hypothesis: conscious information is proposed to be physically integrated in the brain's endogenous electromagnetic field [@mcfadden2002a; @mcfadden2002b]. That proposal remains debated. Evidence that extracellular fields can modulate neuronal activity supports the plausibility of field effects in principle, not the full CEMI theory [@anastassiou2011ephaptic; @jefferys1995nonsynaptic].

At whole-brain scale many microscopic facts are averaged away. One does not track each vesicle or each ion channel. The variables become source distributions, phase relations, frequency bands, coupling matrices, boundary conditions, and behavioural or report measures. Typical data come from EEG, MEG, intracranial recordings, stimulation studies, lesion evidence, imaging, and task reports, each with different spatial and temporal limits. Consciousness theories such as global workspace and integrated information provide other frameworks for the same scale [@baars1988cognitive; @dehaene2014code; @tononi2004iit].

## The [T]-Theory reading

The programme uses the CEMI layer as an `open-hypothesis`: a candidate brain-scale field in which local circuit responses are integrated into a whole-brain propagator. The substrate is neural tissue and its endogenous electromagnetic field; the field variable is a Green's-function response constrained by cortical boundaries and source currents. In *The Missing Limbic Layer: A Somatic Field Extension of Hopfield Networks via the Correspondence Principle*, Hopfield attractors are read `interpretive`ly as emotional basins, not as established diagnoses. Threshold-consciousness claims remain `open-hypothesis`, even where the Lean surface contains `kernel-verified` real-number dichotomies. This level carries up distributed phase, amplitude, and source geometry. The level does not claim that CEMI is proved, that trauma therapy follows, or that a magnetic field alone is a person.
