## In ordinary science

The molecular level begins when atoms form stable arrangements through shared, transferred, or correlated electron density. Typical lengths are tenths of a nanometre for bonds and nanometres for small biomolecules. Bond energies are usually electron-volts, thermal energies at room temperature are about $k_BT\approx0.026$ eV, and vibrational motions often occur on femtosecond to picosecond timescales. Molecular shape matters because the electron distribution, nuclear positions, and surrounding environment jointly set the Hamiltonian [@atkins2014physicalchemistry].

The registry displays the compact quantum relation
$$H\psi=E\psi.$$
In words, $H$ is the Hamiltonian operator encoding kinetic energy and interactions, $\psi$ is the molecular wavefunction or approximate electronic state, and $E$ is an allowed energy. In practical chemistry, the Born-Oppenheimer approximation often separates fast electronic motion from slower nuclear motion, allowing chemists to compute potential-energy surfaces for molecular geometry [@bornoppenheimer1927].

Chemical bonds are not tiny rods. A covalent bond is a lower-energy arrangement of electron density shared between nuclei; ionic bonding is dominated by electrostatic attraction; hydrogen bonding and van der Waals interactions are weaker but crucial for liquids, crystals, proteins, and DNA. Molecular orbitals, density functional theory, spectroscopy, diffraction, cryo-electron microscopy, and molecular dynamics all measure or model different aspects of this level.

Molecules have conformations: distinct shapes separated by energy barriers. Butane rotates between staggered and eclipsed forms; proteins fold into metastable structures; retinal in rhodopsin isomerises after absorbing light. These states can store biochemical information in the ordinary sense that a shape changes binding, catalysis, or signalling. That is not consciousness. It is physical chemistry: stable or metastable minima on an energy surface, transitions driven by heat, photons, collisions, solvent, or enzymes.

This level is where atomic spectra become matter with function. The upper variables are bonds, angles, torsions, charge distributions, conformers, reaction coordinates, and rates. The lower variables--all individual electron and nuclear degrees of freedom--are still present, but most explanations use effective molecular coordinates.

## The [T]-Theory reading

The atlas reads the molecular level as a configurable response surface `interpretive`. The substrate is chemical bond and conformation; the field is electron-density response; an impulse such as a photon, collision, or ligand contact returns a propagated change through orbitals and geometry `derived-under-assumptions`.

What is carried up from atoms is quantised electromagnetic structure and state discreteness `interpretive`. What is integrated out is the full electronic many-body problem when a molecule is represented by bonds, torsions, and conformational basins `derived-under-assumptions`. The programme may compare conformational memory with later attractor language, but that comparison is explicitly a model analogy, not evidence for feelings in molecules `interpretive`. Any cross-scale claim that the same response grammar supports biological or somatic dynamics remains an open research proposal `open-hypothesis`.
