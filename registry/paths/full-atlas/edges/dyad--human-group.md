The zoom from dyad to human group keeps coupling, delay, and boundary discipline, but the display no longer centres one named relation `interpretive`. A dyad can be drawn as $G_{AB}$ and $G_{BA}$. A group needs a coupling matrix $K_{ij}(t-\tau)$, shared sources, density, membership, roles, and media because each participant may respond to many others and to the same external signal `derived-under-assumptions`. The upper level therefore adds variables for choir pulse, meeting procedure, audience attention, crowd spacing, family routine, or team role, depending on the path `interpretive`.

What survives is the response grammar: a perturbation in one part of the social field can alter timing, attention, posture, speech, or movement elsewhere `interpretive`. What is integrated out is the privileged pair display. Individual persons and dyads still exist, but the atlas hides most pair-by-pair detail unless it changes the group pattern `derived-under-assumptions`. A common source can imitate mutual influence, so the edge must distinguish synchrony through coupling from synchrony through a conductor, loudspeaker, ritual script, or alarm `open-hypothesis`.

The level's discipline is anti-fusion. The group propagates, coordinates, amplifies, and sometimes blocks response; it does not become one feeling subject `open-hypothesis`. The renderer should draw many bounded persons linked by selective channels, with slow norm or role boundaries around the group. It should not draw a group mind, aura, or merged consciousness `interpretive`.
