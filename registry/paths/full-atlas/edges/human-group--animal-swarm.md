The zoom from human group to animal swarm is a scale-neighbour comparison on the atlas spine, not a claim that crowds and swarms are the same social object `interpretive`. What is shared is collective coordination: many bounded agents respond locally, and the visible pattern can be a wave, turn, pulse, lane, or direction change `derived-under-assumptions`. The group-level display already has density, timing, and many-to-many coupling. The swarm display keeps those coordination questions but changes the state variables to position, velocity, alignment, cohesion, separation, neighbour radius, noise, and boundary pressure `derived-under-assumptions`.

What is not shared must be said plainly. Human groups carry language, role, ritual, memory, authority, legitimacy, meaning, norm, and institutional recognition `interpretive`. An animal swarm or engineered active-matter swarm may show distributed choice or collective motion, but the atlas must not import parliamentary procedure, religious meaning, musical intention, or moral obligation into fish, birds, insects, drones, or agents `open-hypothesis`. Conversely, animal swarm equations should not be used to explain human crowds while erasing power, fear, policing, architecture, or consent `open-hypothesis`.

The edge therefore abstracts downward in semantic richness while moving to a clean coordination physics. It preserves response grammar and collective pattern, integrates out human meanings and norms, and adds active-matter variables. The renderer should show the human assembly dissolving into agent traces and velocity arrows, with a warning boundary that meaning has been stripped, not solved `interpretive`.
