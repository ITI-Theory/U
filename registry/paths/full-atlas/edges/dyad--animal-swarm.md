The zoom from dyad to animal swarm replaces pairwise coupling as the main display with many local couplings acting at once `interpretive`. The dyadic ideas of delay, detuning, and phase-locking survive as nearest-neighbour or local-network interactions `interpretive`. What changes is the need for density, velocity, alignment pressure, noise, and boundary variables, because pair labels are no longer sufficient for a group turn, school formation, milling pattern, or escape wave `derived-under-assumptions`.

Individual histories and full organism states are averaged into agent rules and field coefficients `derived-under-assumptions`. This averaging is useful only when the chosen variables still predict the collective pattern. If leadership, kinship, hunger, species-specific signalling, or terrain dominates the motion, those variables must either enter the model or limit the claim `open-hypothesis`. The upper level gains explanatory power for collective motion, but it loses the dyad's named participants and much of their individual context `interpretive`.

The response grammar survives as perturbation and propagation: a predator, neighbour turn, obstacle, or environmental change can spread through many local interactions `interpretive`. The result is active matter or collective behaviour, not a group subject, animal collective consciousness, or imported human affect state `open-hypothesis`. The edge should therefore be tested against tracked positions and headings, not against visual resemblance alone `derived-under-assumptions`.
