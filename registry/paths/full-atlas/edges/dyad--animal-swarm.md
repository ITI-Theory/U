The zoom from dyad to animal swarm replaces pairwise coupling as the main display with many local couplings acting at once. The dyadic ideas of delay, detuning, and phase-locking survive as nearest-neighbour or local-network interactions `interpretive`. What changes is the need for density, velocity, alignment pressure, noise, and boundary variables, because pair labels are no longer sufficient for a group turn or school formation `derived-under-assumptions`. Individual histories and full organism states are averaged into agent rules and field coefficients. The upper level gains explanatory power for collective motion, but it loses person-by-person detail. The result is active matter, not a group subject or animal collective consciousness `open-hypothesis`.
