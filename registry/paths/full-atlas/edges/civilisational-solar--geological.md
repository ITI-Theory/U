The zoom from civilisational/solar to geological is a boundary marker on the full atlas spine. It is where the ladder leaves the social and hybrid systemic band and returns to physical Earth scale `interpretive`. Civilisational variables include records, law, energy systems, trade, demography, and institutional memory. Solar-scale variables include gravity, orbit, radiation, and long-horizon trajectory. Geological variables instead require rock stress, elastic stiffness, fault geometry, plate motion, deformation history, seismic velocity, erosion, sedimentation, and stratigraphic archive `derived-under-assumptions`.

What survives is long-timescale constraint. A civilisation, an orbit, and a fault system can all filter perturbations through inherited structure, but the mechanisms are different `interpretive`. The edge therefore preserves response grammar only at the abstract level: impulse, boundary, delay, storage, release, and propagation. It integrates out social meaning, institutional legitimacy, political agency, and orbital display priority. It adds the elastic and tectonic variables that make an earthquake, fold, thrust, basin, or mountain belt measurable `derived-under-assumptions`.

This edge should not be read as a claim that rocks have institutions or that societies are geological plates `open-hypothesis`. It is a navigation turn. The renderer should move from maps of institutions or orbital basins to crustal sections, faults, stress arrows, seismic wavefronts, and strata. The lens-on overlay may keep memory and propagation labels, but the substrate is now rock, not culture or solar-system motion `interpretive`.
