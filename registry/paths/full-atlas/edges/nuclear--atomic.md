Zooming from nuclear to atomic scales moves from confined short-range strong response to electromagnetic structure around a charged nucleus. What survives is discreteness: stable configurations, transition amplitudes, and measured spectra `interpretive`. What is averaged away is most quark and gluon detail; chemistry usually retains nuclear charge, isotope mass, spin, and magnetic moments as effective inputs `derived-under-assumptions`. The upper level needs electron orbitals, Coulomb screening, ionisation energy, and photon transitions because atoms are governed mainly by electromagnetic response at angstrom distances. The bridge does not make nuclei into atoms by analogy; it records a physical hierarchy in which nuclear charge supplies the source for the atomic electron field `empirical-result`.
