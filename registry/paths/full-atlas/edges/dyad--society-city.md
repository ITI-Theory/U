A dyad can coordinate by turn-taking, repair, imitation, entrainment, trust, and shared expectation `empirical-result`. When the view zooms to society-city, those two-person episodes no longer appear as named relationships. They survive as repeated practices, etiquette, norms, meeting places, markets, transit habits, dialects, media channels, and institutions `interpretive`. What is preserved is response grammar: impulse, delay, amplification, damping, and repair. What is averaged away is biography, private history, and the full conversational sequence `derived-under-assumptions`.

The upper level needs new variables because a city can stabilise or block a practice after the originating dyads have disappeared. Density changes encounter rate; infrastructure changes path length; law and pricing change access; archives and schedules preserve routines; inequality changes who can transmit or receive a practice. A handshake, chant, rumour, fashion, or care routine becomes urban only when many dyads repeat it through durable channels. `interpretive`: The claim is not that the city becomes a person. It is that dyadic coordination can be institutionalised into visible collective response.

This is also why simple aggregation is insufficient. Ten thousand dyads in separate rooms do not yet make a city-scale field. The transition requires shared surfaces: streets, platforms, workplaces, schools, ritual spaces, public records, media, and enforceable rules. `open-hypothesis`: A proposed city-level reading should therefore identify which dyadic pattern survives and which urban channel preserves it.
