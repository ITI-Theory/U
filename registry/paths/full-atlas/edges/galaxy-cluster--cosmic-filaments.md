At galaxy-cluster scale the object is a bound node with galaxies, hot gas, dark matter, lensing mass, shocks, and merger history. At cosmic-filament scale that node becomes part of a larger network of bridges, sheets, voids, and inflow channels `empirical-result`. The cluster is no longer the whole object; it is a junction or endpoint within an anisotropic matter field.

What survives is the response evidence: total mass, gas pressure, galaxy velocities, lensing, radio plasma, and merger state. These become node properties or boundary conditions for the filamentary environment `derived-under-assumptions`. What is integrated out is the detailed identity of member galaxies, the full temperature map of each cluster, and most internal merger morphology. The upper level instead needs topology, connectivity, linear density, filament width, node degree, void boundary, velocity flow, warm-hot gas content, and tracer bias.

`interpretive`: The Atlas reads the transition as a move from separated component response to channelled large-scale response. In a cluster, gas, galaxies, and dark matter can visibly separate; in a filament, the main question is how matter and galaxies are arranged along an extended bridge. This does not turn filaments into nerves or intentions. It states that the same gravitational history becomes a different display when connectivity rather than one bound node is the organising variable.
