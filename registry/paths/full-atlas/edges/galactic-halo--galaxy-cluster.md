A galactic halo is the extended environment of one galaxy. A galaxy cluster is a bound system of many galaxies, many haloes, hot intracluster plasma, dark matter, diffuse light, shocks, and lensing mass `empirical-result`. The transition therefore changes the meaning of "environment". A halo surrounds a galaxy; a cluster contains multiple galaxies and haloes inside a larger potential well whose components respond differently to the same merger or accretion history.

What survives is indirect mass inference. Rotation, streams, satellites, lensing, and gas all remain response channels, but they are now joined by cluster-scale observables: richness, velocity dispersion, X-ray temperature, Sunyaev-Zel'dovich pressure, lensing convergence, gas fraction, merger state, and mass within conventional radii such as $R_{500}$ `empirical-result`. What is integrated out is most internal disc, spiral, star-forming, and single-halo substructure. Individual galaxies become tracers and baryonic subcomponents unless the cluster question requires their morphology or history.

`interpretive`: The Atlas reads this step as accretion plus response separation. Collisionless galaxies and dark matter, collisional plasma, radio-emitting particles, and background light do not return identical signals. The upper level needs new variables precisely because the responses separate. A cluster is not a larger galaxy and not a collective subject; it is a multi-component gravitational and plasma system with labelled evidence channels.
