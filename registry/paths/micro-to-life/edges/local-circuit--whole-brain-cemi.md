Local circuits become a whole-brain CEMI candidate when many neighbourhood responses are coupled through long-range anatomy, recurrent loops, tissue conductivity, and volume conduction `open-hypothesis`. The surviving quantities are source currents, phase relations, frequency bands, coupling matrices, propagation delays, and boundary conditions `interpretive`. The averaged-away details are the exact cellular route by which each local current was produced: which vesicle released, which channel opened, and which dendritic branch supplied the current `derived-under-assumptions`.

Whole-brain variables are needed because EEG, MEG, sleep state, anaesthesia, attention, seizure, and reportable behaviour depend on distributed coordination, not on a single cortical column `derived-under-assumptions`. A local oscillation may be important only when it locks to, drives, or is suppressed by a larger network. The electromagnetic field is an `empirical-result` as a measurable brain-generated signal; McFadden-style CEMI consciousness is an `open-hypothesis`.

The [T]-Theory propagator reading is `interpretive`: local Green responses are promoted into a brain-scale response constrained by anatomy and field boundaries. It does not claim that consciousness, clinical change, or trauma dynamics have been proved by field geometry. The transition remains testable only if field-level variables add causal or predictive power beyond ordinary source activity and connectivity `open-hypothesis`.
