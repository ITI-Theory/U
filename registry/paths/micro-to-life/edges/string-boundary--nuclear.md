Zooming from the string boundary to the nuclear level replaces worldsheet and compactification variables with hadrons, nuclei, scattering amplitudes, binding energies, and decay channels `interpretive`. The general response pattern survives: a source is prepared, a kernel or interaction law governs propagation, and an outcome distribution is measured `interpretive`. What is averaged away is the detailed string-scale spectrum and compact geometry. At the nuclear scale, effective quantum field theory, QCD, and nuclear models work with quarks, gluons, nucleons, mesons, shells, and collective modes `derived-under-assumptions`.

The upper level needs new variables because femtometre experiments do not report string coordinates. They report cross-sections, form factors, half-lives, gamma rays, particle tracks, and masses `empirical-result`. In ordinary physics there is no completed empirical route from M-theory to the observed table of nuclei `open-hypothesis`. The atlas therefore marks "localise / scatter" as a model bridge: response grammar is preserved, but evidence restarts at the measured nuclear variables.
