Cosmic filaments are extended bridges in the matter distribution. The observable universe is the whole causally accessible volume from which signals can reach the observer. Moving between them changes the frame from network features to horizon-limited cosmological sampling `empirical-result`. A filament remains part of the map, but it is now one contribution to correlation functions, power spectra, lensing fields, redshift surveys, CMB comparisons, and expansion-history fits.

What survives is density topology, tracer bias, baryonic gas, dark-matter scaffold, and the record of initial perturbations carried into late structure `derived-under-assumptions`. What is integrated out is the named identity of most individual filaments and nodes. The upper level needs variables that a filament catalogue alone cannot supply: scale factor, redshift, horizon, curvature, optical depth, density parameters, growth rate, survey mask, selection function, and cosmic variance.

`interpretive`: The Atlas reads this as a transition from channel to sampled volume. The response grammar remains: early perturbations, matter-energy contents, and metric expansion set later observables. The evidence boundary tightens because Planck 2018 values and other cosmological probes are model fits, not direct pictures of totality. The programme's $7/11$ and $3/11$ comparisons must therefore be shown only as model-dependent comparisons beside measured cosmological parameters `derived-under-assumptions`, never as empirical derivations of nature.
