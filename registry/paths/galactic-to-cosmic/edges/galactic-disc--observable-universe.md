Zooming from a galactic disc to the observable universe replaces one rotating baryonic system with a causal survey volume `interpretive`. Some structures survive: gravity, density contrast, rotation histories, feedback products, and dark-halo inference all contribute to the large-scale matter map `interpretive`. What is averaged away is almost everything local to a galaxy: spiral-arm microstructure, star-forming clouds, bars, and individual stellar populations become statistical tracers or bias parameters `derived-under-assumptions`. The upper level needs redshift, horizon, expansion history, power spectrum, and density parameters because observations arrive on the past light cone, not from a simultaneous map `derived-under-assumptions`. Cosmological [T]-Theory ratios such as $7/11$ and $3/11$ are model-dependent comparisons beside Planck 2018, not measurements `derived-under-assumptions`.

The transition also changes the meaning of a picture. A disc image can be close to a present-tense morphology: arms, gas, dust, and stars arranged in one bound system. A universe image is a reconstruction across look-back time, survey masks, tracer bias, and model assumptions `derived-under-assumptions`. The ordinary operation is therefore compression: many galaxies become a correlation function, a luminosity function, a halo occupation model, or a pixel in a density field. The [T]-Theory operation is also compression, but must be labelled as response grammar rather than observation `interpretive`. If the overlay cannot state what is preserved, what is lost, and what would falsify the comparison, it remains an atlas guide only `open-hypothesis`.
