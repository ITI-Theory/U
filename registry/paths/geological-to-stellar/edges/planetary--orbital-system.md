At planetary scale the body is a layered system: interior, mantle, crust, ocean or volatile layer, atmosphere, magnetosphere, and radiative balance. At orbital-system scale that same body is usually reduced to a moving source of gravity with mass, radius, spin, quadrupole, albedo, drag, and tidal response `derived-under-assumptions`. The planet does not stop being geophysical, but most of its weather, rock history, and internal circulation become secondary unless they alter the orbit, torque, transit, occultation, or spacecraft range.

What survives is response grammar. A mass perturbs other masses; an impulse changes phase, eccentricity, inclination, or spin; tides carry delayed deformation from one body into another `empirical-result`. What is added is the barycentric grammar of the whole system: ephemerides, resonances, mean-motion locks, stability zones, ring gaps, comet tails, asteroid families, and the distinction between a body's internal physics and its effective gravitational signature.

`interpretive`: The Atlas transition is a disciplined coarse-graining. The lower level keeps whole-planet coupling visible; the upper level asks which planetary variables survive as orbital sources and boundary conditions. Surface detail is integrated out into effective mass, multipoles, drag, radiation response, and tidal dissipation. No awareness or planetary intention is implied. The new variables are needed because a system of mutual free fall cannot be described by one planet's fluid or solid response alone.
