At orbital-system scale the main variables are trajectories, barycentres, resonances, tides, and perturbations among bodies. At stellar scale the display moves into one dominant body whose gravity, pressure, opacity, nuclear reactions, convection, rotation, magnetic field, and surface radiation form a self-regulating plasma system `empirical-result`. The shift is not from gravity to something non-physical. Gravity remains essential, but it is now balanced against pressure and energy transport inside an object that produces light.

What survives is mass, angular momentum, composition history, accretion, binary forcing, and tidal or rotational memory. These become stellar boundary conditions rather than orbit diagrams `derived-under-assumptions`. What is averaged away is most of the system geometry: minor bodies, orbital debris, and companion paths matter only when they measurably change rotation, accretion, oscillation, or evolution.

The upper level adds variables that cannot be recovered from Keplerian elements alone. A star needs central temperature, density, pressure, opacity, luminosity, chemical abundance, convection-zone depth, magnetic activity, neutrino flux, and mode spectrum. `interpretive`: The Atlas reads the transition as source-response retyping: external gravitational history and material supply enter a plasma cavity, and the returned signal is spectrum, luminosity, wind, flare, or oscillation. This is not a claim that stars feel. It is a claim that a new substrate requires a new response kernel.
