At geological scale the focus is a fault, fold, basin, thrust sheet, volcanic arc, or rock volume: stress is stored locally and released through deformation or seismic waves `empirical-result`. At planetary scale those details become part of a coupled heat, mass, momentum, and field budget `derived-under-assumptions`. Individual structures do not vanish, but they are no longer the display priority. They are averaged into lithospheric strength, heat flux, topography, viscosity, plate boundary conditions, volatile inventory, and interior structure.

What survives is impulse-response grammar. Earthquakes, mantle plumes, impacts, volcanic gases, solar forcing, and tidal stress all enter media that filter, delay, and couple the response. What is added is whole-planet coupling: rotation, gravity, stratification, oceans, atmospheres, magnetic fields, radiation, ice, and space weather. Mantle convection, plate motion, ocean circulation, atmospheric waves, and magnetic response are measured geophysical processes `empirical-result`. `interpretive`: The upper level needs new variables because a planet is not just more rock; it is a nested solid-fluid-electromagnetic system with feedbacks across interior, surface, atmosphere, ocean, and space environment.

The evidence changes with the zoom. Geological mapping can follow one fault or basin; planetary analysis must combine seismology, geodesy, satellites, ocean floats, weather stations, magnetometers, and spacecraft data. `open-hypothesis`: A valid Atlas transition must state which local structures became boundary conditions and which planetary couplings newly control the response.
