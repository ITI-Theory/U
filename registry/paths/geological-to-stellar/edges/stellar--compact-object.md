At stellar scale the object is a luminous plasma whose long-lived balance depends on gravity, pressure, nuclear energy generation, opacity, convection, and magnetic activity. At compact-object scale that balance has ended or transformed. The remnant keeps only a compressed subset of the stellar history: mass, spin, composition, magnetic field, binary environment, and collapse or cooling path `derived-under-assumptions`. Ordinary envelopes, sustained hydrogen fusion, and most surface weather are no longer the display priority.

The new level adds boundary physics. White dwarfs require electron degeneracy pressure; neutron stars require nuclear-density matter and uncertain equations of state; black holes require causal horizons and spacetime curvature `empirical-result`. If the compact object is disturbed, its response may appear as pulsar timing residuals, X-ray variability, tidal deformability, accretion signatures, or gravitational-wave ringdown. The registry kernel therefore changes from stellar mode selection to damped quasi-normal response.

`interpretive`: The Atlas preserves impulse-response grammar while tightening the boundary conditions. A star can oscillate throughout a plasma body; a black-hole merger returns a sparse ringdown; a neutron star may reveal interior stiffness through tides and glitches. The transition is not a metaphysical elevation. It is a coarse-graining from extended stellar life to dense remnant parameters, where very few observables carry a large amount of inferred history.
