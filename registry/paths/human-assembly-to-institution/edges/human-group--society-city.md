The Performance Ensemble / Audience path zooms from a room-scale group into the city that hosts, funds, routes, advertises, licenses, remembers, and interprets the event `interpretive`. At the human-group level, the important variables may be pulse, breath, attention, cueing, applause, sight line, and call-response. At society/city scale, those moment-by-moment variables are still real but no longer the main display. The city adds venue networks, public transport, ticketing, media channels, rehearsal spaces, noise rules, safety codes, archives, markets, schools, festivals, and informal scenes `derived-under-assumptions`.

What survives is response grammar. A performance can perturb an audience; repeated performances can perturb a scene; a scene can change routes, institutions, repertoires, funding, and civic memory `interpretive`. What is integrated out is most live ensemble detail unless it becomes a city-scale trace: attendance, recordings, reviews, shared repertoire, training pipeline, protest, ritual calendar, or institutional change `derived-under-assumptions`. A spectacular concert and a weekly rehearsal may have different kernels because one propagates through mass media while the other propagates through practice and membership `open-hypothesis`.

The edge must not treat the city as a larger audience or the audience as a miniature city `open-hypothesis`. It embeds performance in infrastructure and institution. The renderer should widen the stage view into streets, venues, schools, networks, calendars, screens, and archives, with response waves travelling through channels rather than through a single fused crowd `interpretive`.
