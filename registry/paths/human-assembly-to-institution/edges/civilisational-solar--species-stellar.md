Civilisational structures and solar-system basins are not carried upward as literal objects. They are integrated out into longer-lived variables: lineage, extinction risk, genetic variation, population branching, ecological exposure, stellar radiation, oscillation modes, chemical composition, and neighbourhood history `derived-under-assumptions`. What is averaged away is the named empire, settlement system, institution, individual planet, or orbital configuration that dominated the previous display. What is added is branch-specific. On the species side, selection, drift, migration, reproductive isolation, and extinction become necessary. On the stellar side, plasma structure, radiation transport, normal modes, mass loss, magnetic activity, and astrometric neighbourhoods become necessary. `open-hypothesis`: The Atlas does not claim evolution and stellar physics are the same science. Written records and orbital ephemerides give way to genomes, fossils, spectra, light curves, parallaxes, and stellar models. `interpretive`: The shared term "branch" must be redefined locally: lineage branch in biology, mode or evolutionary branch in stellar work.
