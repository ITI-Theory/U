The zoom from bird to flock keeps the bounded avian body but stops treating one bird as the whole scene `interpretive`. The individual force-balance variables--position, heading, speed, body orientation, aerodynamic forcing, sensory delay, and response to gusts--survive as agent state `interpretive`. The upper level adds neighbour alignment, density, collision avoidance, turn propagation, shared airspace, group boundary, and landscape constraints `derived-under-assumptions`.

Wingbeat mechanics, feather detail, muscle physiology, and most internal state are integrated out unless they affect a flock-scale coefficient, constraint, or response latency `derived-under-assumptions`. This is a change of explanatory target, not a denial that those details matter. A flock turn cannot be represented by one bird's Newton equation alone, because the visible pattern depends on how many bounded birds update from local cues and how their updates propagate `interpretive`.

The resulting avian active-matter field can show how a local perturbation spreads through many birds `interpretive`. It must still respect the bird level: flight speed, aerodynamic limits, sensory occlusion, fatigue, and species-specific behaviour constrain the field `derived-under-assumptions`. It should be calibrated against tracked birds when possible, because similar silhouettes can hide different species rules `open-hypothesis`. The zoom does not claim that the flock is one animal, that it has a mind, or that a generic swarm equation is automatically valid for every bird species `open-hypothesis`.
