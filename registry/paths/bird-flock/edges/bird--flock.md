The zoom from bird to flock keeps the bounded avian body but stops treating one bird as the whole scene. The individual force-balance variables--position, heading, speed, aerodynamic forcing, and response to gusts--survive as agent state `interpretive`. The upper level adds neighbour alignment, density, collision avoidance, turn propagation, and shared airspace variables `derived-under-assumptions`. Wingbeat mechanics, feather detail, and most internal physiology are integrated out unless they affect the flock-scale coefficient or constraint. The resulting avian active-matter field can show how a local perturbation spreads through many birds. It is a collective-motion model, not a claim that the flock is one animal `open-hypothesis`.
