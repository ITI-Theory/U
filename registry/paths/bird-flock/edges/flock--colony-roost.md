The zoom from flock to colony-roost changes the display from moving alignment to site-scale continuity `interpretive`. The flock velocity field survives as $\mathbf v_b$, but it is retyped as population transport through a habitat or roost domain `derived-under-assumptions`. The upper level adds bird density $\rho_b$, habitat boundary, arrival and departure rates, daily timing, seasonal recurrence, disturbance, and optional sources or sinks `derived-under-assumptions`.

Individual trajectories, wingbeats, momentary turn waves, and local neighbour choices are averaged into population flow `derived-under-assumptions`. This is necessary because a roost can persist across many separate flights and many changing individuals `empirical-result`. The upper-level question is no longer only how a turn spreads through air, but where birds accumulate, how density changes, and how routine becomes a site pattern `interpretive`.

The edge also changes the meaning of memory. It is recurrence, site fidelity, and measurable continuity of use, not recollection by a colony subject `interpretive`. The continuity equation should be supplemented by source and sink terms when births, deaths, immigration, emigration, predation, or measurement losses matter `derived-under-assumptions`. Counts, radar, tags, and acoustic records can disagree, so uncertainty belongs in the site-scale display and should be shown with the density estimate `derived-under-assumptions`. The model does not infer colony intention, teleology, or collective subjectivity `open-hypothesis`.
