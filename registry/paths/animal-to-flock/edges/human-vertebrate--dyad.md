The zoom from human/vertebrate to dyad turns one bounded organism into one term of a two-body coupling problem `interpretive`. What survives is the organism's response grammar: inputs, damping, memory, attractor bias, and noise `interpretive`. What is added is the off-diagonal kernel $G_{AB}(t-\tau)$, because one body now appears as delayed forcing for the other `derived-under-assumptions`. The reverse direction may have a different kernel, delay, and strength, so $G_{BA}$ should not be assumed identical `derived-under-assumptions`.

The dyad needs variables for phase, delay, coupling strength, channel, task, context, and boundary preservation `derived-under-assumptions`. The internal details of each person or animal are not erased, but they are integrated out of the dyad display unless they affect measured coupling `interpretive`. Music, speech, gaze, posture, spacing, call-response, and shared external rhythms can be carriers `interpretive`. A common driver can imitate coupling, so the edge must distinguish mutual influence from both bodies responding to the same stimulus `open-hypothesis`.

The zoom's main discipline is separation. Two organismal fields are drawn with a link between them, not fused into one field `interpretive`. The upper level gains relational observables and loses person-by-person internal detail. It does not claim telepathy, merged consciousness, therapy, diagnosis, or guaranteed regulation `open-hypothesis`.
