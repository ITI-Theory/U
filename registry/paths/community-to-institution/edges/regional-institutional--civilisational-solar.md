The regional level tracks bounded propagation through geography and institutions `interpretive`. The civilisational-solar level asks what persists when many regions are coupled for long periods, or when the physical branch is read as orbital structure around a star `interpretive`. Local jurisdictions, towns, watersheds, service areas, and corridors are integrated out into larger attractors: energy regimes, archives, trade networks, imperial or federal structures, standards, military reach, or, on the physical branch, gravitational basins and resonances `derived-under-assumptions`.

What survives is response grammar and path dependence. What changes is the state space. A regional shock may be described by travel time, authority, and service catchment; a civilisational shock also involves archive depth, resource base, ideological legitimacy, demography, energy infrastructure, and interregional dependence. On the solar branch, the variables are mass, potential, orbital elements, resonance, and perturbation. `open-hypothesis`: The hybrid level is not a proof that societies and solar systems share a substance. It needs new variables because long-horizon stability depends on records, resource flows, gravitational wells, or basin structure, not merely on regional diffusion speed.

The averaging is therefore asymmetric. On the social branch, many regions become a historical system with memory in institutions and material supply. On the physical branch, many bodies become a dynamical system with memory in orbital elements and resonances. `interpretive`: The bridge is basin persistence, not a shared mechanism.
