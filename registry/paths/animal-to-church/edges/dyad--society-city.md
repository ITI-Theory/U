A dyad can coordinate by turn-taking, repair, imitation, and shared expectation. At city scale those local couplings no longer appear as two named people. They survive as repeated practices, norms, meeting places, routes, media channels, and institutions. `interpretive`: The transition keeps the response grammar--impulse, delay, amplification, damping--but averages away biography into encounter rates and network structure. `derived-under-assumptions`: The upper level needs variables for density, infrastructure, law, memory, and unequal access because a city can stabilise or block a practice after the originating dyads have disappeared. The claim is not that the city becomes a person; it is that dyadic coordination can be institutionalised.
